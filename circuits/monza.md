# Autodromo Nazionale Monza — History & Statistics

The **Autodromo Nazionale Monza**, located near the city of Monza, north of Milan in Italy, is the historic "Temple of Speed" and one of the world's oldest purpose-built motor racing circuits. Built in 1922 in the royal park (*Parco di Monza*), Monza has hosted the Italian Grand Prix in every World Championship season with the exception of 1980 (held at Imola). It is the fastest circuit on the Formula One calendar.

## Circuit Specifications

- **Location**: Monza, Lombardy, Italy (45°37′14″N, 09°16′52″W)
- **Current Track Length**: 5.793 km (3.600 mi)
- **Turns**: 11 (Variante del Rettifilo, Curva Grande, Variante della Roggia, Lesmo 1 & 2, Variante Ascari, Curva Parabolica / Curva Alboreto)
- **Race Distance**: 53 laps (306.720 km / 190.587 mi)
- **Official Race Lap Record**: 
  - **1:21.046** — Rubens Barrichello, Scuderia Ferrari (Ferrari F2004, 2004 Italian Grand Prix, Lap 41).
  - *Source*: FIA Official Classification [2004 Italian GP](https://www.formula1.com/en/results.html/2004/races/767/italy/fastest-laps.html)
- **Fastest Formula 1 Lap in History (Average Speed)**:
  - **1:18.887 (264.362 km/h / 164.267 mph average speed)** — Lewis Hamilton, Mercedes F1 W11 EQ Performance (2020 Q3 Pole Lap, 2020-09-05).
  - *Source*: FIA Official Qualifying Classification [2020 Italian GP](https://www.formula1.com/en/results.html/2020/races/1052/italy/qualifying.html)

## Historical Eras & Layouts
1. **1922–1954**: Initial combination of 5.5 km road circuit and 4.5 km high-speed banked oval.
2. **1955–1961 (Combined 10 km Oval & Road Course)**: 10.000 km layout featured banked oval concrete banking (*sopraelevata*). Used in World Championship Grands Prix in 1955, 1956, 1960, and 1961. Abandoned after Wolfgang von Trips' fatal crash in 1961.
3. **1962–1971 (Pure Slipstreaming Road Course)**: High-speed slipstream battles without chicanes. 1971 Italian GP produced the closest finish in Formula 1 history: Peter Gethin won by 0.01 seconds over Ronnie Peterson, with top 5 separated by 0.61 seconds.
4. **1972–Present (Chicane Era)**: Chicanes added at Rettifilo, Roggia, and Vialone (now Ascari) to reduce terminal speeds into corners. Modified to current configuration in 2000.
