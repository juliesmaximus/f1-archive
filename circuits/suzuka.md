# Circuit Profile: Suzuka International Racing Course

## Overview & The Temple of Pure Driving
The **Suzuka International Racing Course** (鈴鹿サーキット, *Suzuka Sākitto*), situated in Mie Prefecture, Japan, is universally regarded by Grand Prix drivers and engineers as one of the ultimate tests of human skill and aerodynamic capability in all of motorsport.

Designed in 1962 by Dutch track architect **John Hugenholtz** as an experimental test track for the Honda Motor Company, Suzuka is famous for its unique **figure-eight layout**, where the back straight passes over the front section via a bridge and underpass. Joining the FIA Formula One World Championship calendar in **1987**, Suzuka quickly established itself as the sport's definitive championship crucible, deciding no fewer than twelve World Drivers' Championships between 1987 and 2003.

## Circuit Specifications
- **Location:** Suzuka City, Mie Prefecture, Kansai, Japan
- **Coordinates:** 34°50'35\"N, 136°32'26\"E
- **Circuit Length:** 5.859 km (1987) / 5.807 km (Modern layout)
- **Turns:** 18
- **Direction:** Clockwise (Figure-Eight with crossover)
- **Elevation Change:** 40.4 metres (132.5 feet)

## Legendary Corners & Flow
1. **First & Second Curves (Turns 1 & 2):** Fast downhill double-apex right-hander immediately following the start/finish straight.
2. **The "S" Curves (Turns 3–6):** An exquisite, rhythm-dependent uphill series of left-right-left-right flowing corners where downforce, steering accuracy, and throttle modulation are tested to absolute perfection.
3. **Dunlop Curve (Turn 7):** Long, blind uphill left-hand sweeper leading onto the crest.
4. **Degner Curves (Turns 8 & 9):** Named after motorcycle racer Ernst Degner; a fast blind right-hander immediately followed by a tight right-hand 90-degree bend over high kerbs.
5. **The Hairpin (Turn 11):** Slow, heavily banked 180-degree left-hander offering prime overtaking.
6. **Spoon Curve (Turns 13 & 14):** Demanding downhill double-apex left-hander requiring early throttle application onto the back straight.
7. **130R (Turn 15):** The most terrifying corner on the calendar: a blind, flat-out 130-metre-radius left-hander taken at over **310 km/h (193 mph)** pulling over 4.5G of lateral load.
8. **Casio Triangle (Turns 16–18):** Heavy braking chicane leading back onto the pit straight.

## Historic World Championship Deciders
- **1987:** Nigel Mansell crashed violently at the S-Curves in qualifying, suffering spinal concussions and withdrawing, handing the World Championship to Williams teammate Nelson Piquet.
- **1988 (Senna's Maiden Title):** Ayrton Senna stalled on the grid, dropped to 14th, and mounted a legendary recovery drive through rain to pass Alain Prost on lap 28 and claim his **first World Drivers' Championship**.
- **1989 (The Collision at the Chicane):** Alain Prost and Ayrton Senna collided at the Casio Triangle on lap 47. Prost retired; Senna restarted through the chicane escape road, won the race, but was controversially disqualified by FISA, handing the title to Prost.
- **1990 (The 160 MPH Turn 1 Revenge):** In the greatest act of retribution in F1 history, Ayrton Senna deliberately rammed Alain Prost's Ferrari at 160 mph into Turn 1 at the start, eliminating both cars and clinching his **second World Championship**.
- **1998 & 1999:** Mika Häkkinen won back-to-back season finales to capture his two World Championships for McLaren-Mercedes.
- **2000 (Ferrari's 21-Year Holy Grail):** Michael Schumacher drove a rain-slicked tactical masterpiece to defeat Mika Häkkinen, ending Scuderia Ferrari's 21-year drought for the World Drivers' Championship.

## Grand Prix Statistics & Records
- **Most Wins (Driver):** Michael Schumacher (6 victories: 1995, 1997, 2000, 2001, 2002, 2004); Lewis Hamilton (5 victories).
- **Most Wins (Constructor):** McLaren (9 victories), Ferrari (7 victories).
- **Official F1 Race Lap Record:** 1:30.983 (Lewis Hamilton, Mercedes W10, 2019).
