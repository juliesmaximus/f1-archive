# Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez — Mexico City Grand Prix Circuit History & Archive

The **Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez** in Mexico City is the highest-altitude circuit on the FIA Formula One World Championship calendar, located in the Magdalena Mixhuca public park in eastern Mexico City. It has hosted 24 World Championship Grands Prix across three distinct eras (1963–1970, 1986–1992, 2015–present), having crowned multiple World Champions and witnessed some of the most iconic racing moments in Formula 1 history.

---

## 1. Circuit Specifications & Technical Nuances

- **Circuit Name**: Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez (originally *Circuito Magdalena Mixhuca*)
- **Location**: Mexico City, Mexico (19°24′15″N, 99°05′33″W)
- **Current Track Length**: 4.304 km (2.674 mi)
- **Turns**: 17 (10 right, 7 left)
- **Race Distance**: 71 laps (305.354 km / 189.738 mi)
- **Elevation**: 2,285 metres (7,497 ft) above sea level
- **Atmospheric Pressure**: ~78.0 kPa (~780 mbar), approximately 20% to 25% lower air density compared to sea-level circuits.

### Atmospheric and Engineering Effects of Altitude:
1. **Aerodynamics**: 
   - Due to the low air density, cars run maximum-downforce wing and flap packages (identical to Monaco or Hungaroring configurations). However, the thin air generates downforce and aerodynamic drag equivalent only to Monza low-downforce setups.
   - Cars achieve extreme top speeds exceeding 350 km/h (217 mph) along the 1.2-kilometre front straight into Turn 1.
2. **Thermal Management & Cooling**:
   - Thinner air carries substantially less mass per volume through cooling ducts and radiators. Teams must run aggressively opened engine louvers, enlarged brake ducts, and high-capacity oil/water heat exchangers to prevent power unit overheating and brake fluid vaporization.
3. **Turbochargers & Power Units**:
   - Modern 1.6-litre turbocharged power units must spin the MGU-H/compressor shaft at or near the regulatory maximum limit (125,000 RPM) to compensate for the lower ambient oxygen and maintain intake manifold pressure.

## 3. Circuit Evolution & Eras

```
Era 1 (1962–1970): 5.000 km | Fast, bumpy, sweeping Peraltada banking
Era 2 (1986–1992): 4.421 km | Redesigned infield, shortened hairpins, banked Peraltada
Era 3 (2015–Present): 4.304 km | Tilke redesign; Peraltada bypassed via Foro Sol stadium
```

### Era 1: Magdalena Mixhuca (1962–1970)
- **1962 (Non-championship)**: Inaugural race marred by tragedy when Mexican prodigy Ricardo Rodríguez was killed during practice at the fearsome Peraltada curve driving a Rob Walker Lotus 24. Lotus drivers Jim Clark and Trevor Taylor shared victory after Clark's car was push-started.
- **1963**: Official World Championship debut. Jim Clark scored a dominant Grand Slam (pole, fastest lap, led every lap, victory) in the Lotus 25-Climax.
- **1964**: Famous title decider. John Surtees (Ferrari 158) finished 2nd to win the Drivers' World Championship by one point over Graham Hill, becoming the first and only person in motorsport history to win premier-class World Championships on two wheels (Motorcycling Grand Prix 500cc) and four wheels (Formula 1).
- **1965**: Richie Ginther drove the Honda RA272 to victory, securing Honda's first-ever Formula One victory and the first Grand Prix win for a Goodyear tyre.
- **1967**: Denny Hulme (Brabham-Repco) finished 3rd behind Jim Clark and Jack Brabham to secure the 1967 World Drivers' Championship.
- **1968**: Graham Hill (Lotus 49B) won the race and clinched his second Drivers' World Championship.
- **1970**: Overcrowding resulted in ~200,000 spectators spilling across the curbs and grass verges. Jackie Stewart struck a dog at high speed, breaking his Tyrrell's suspension. Severe safety issues led the FIA to remove Mexico from the calendar.

### Era 2: Redesigned Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez (1986–1992)
Renamed in honour of brothers Ricardo and Pedro Rodríguez, the circuit returned in 1986 with a reworked layout (4.421 km).
- **1986**: Gerhard Berger earned his maiden Grand Prix victory and the first win for the Benetton team (BMW turbo) by running the entire 68-lap race without changing his Pirelli tyres.
- **1987**: Nigel Mansell won for Williams-Honda in an aggregate two-part race after Derek Warwick's heavy crash at the Peraltada caused a red flag.
- **1988–1989**: McLaren-Honda dominant era; Alain Prost won in 1988, followed by Ayrton Senna's commanding win from pole in 1989.
- **1990**: Widely considered one of the greatest races in F1 history. Alain Prost won for Ferrari from 13th on the grid. On the penultimate lap, Nigel Mansell pulled off an audacious overtake around the outside of Gerhard Berger through the fearsome 180 mph 180-degree banked Peraltada corner to hand Ferrari a 1-2 finish.
- **1991**: Ayrton Senna survived a terrifying rollover crash in the Peraltada during Friday practice. Riccardo Patrese took victory for Williams-Renault ahead of teammate Nigel Mansell.
- **1992**: Nigel Mansell led a dominant Williams 1-2. Future 7-time World Champion Michael Schumacher earned his first-ever career Formula One podium (3rd place) in the Benetton B191B.

### Era 3: Hermann Tilke Modern Era (2015–Present)
Re-engineered by Hermann Tilke, the circuit returned in 2015. To bypass the extremely dangerous outer half of the Peraltada (where modern run-off was impossible due to city roads), the track diverted into the 26,000-seat **Foro Sol** baseball stadium (now *Estadio GNP Seguros*), creating an amphitheatre section before rejoining the final apex of Peraltada (Turn 17, *Curva Mansell*).
- **2015**: Nico Rosberg won the return race for Mercedes from pole position.
- **2016**: Lewis Hamilton won. A heated late-race battle saw Max Verstappen penalized 5 seconds for running off at Turn 1, elevating Sebastian Vettel to the podium; Vettel was subsequently penalized 10 seconds for moving under braking against Daniel Ricciardo, ultimately giving Ricciardo 3rd place hours after the race.
- **2017**: Max Verstappen won dominantly. Despite first-lap contact with Sebastian Vettel that forced both to pit, Lewis Hamilton finished 9th to clinch his 4th World Drivers' Championship.
- **2018**: Max Verstappen won for the second consecutive year. Lewis Hamilton finished 4th to clinch his 5th World Drivers' Championship.
- **2019**: Lewis Hamilton took his second Mexican Grand Prix victory with a masterclass 1-stop tyre strategy.
- **2021**: Max Verstappen executed a breathtaking three-wide slipstream pass around both Mercedes cars (Bottas and Hamilton) on the outside of Turn 1 at the start to win comfortably.
- **2022**: Max Verstappen broke the all-time record for most Grand Prix wins in a single season (14) by taking victory from pole position.
- **2023**: Max Verstappen achieved his 5th Mexican GP victory, extending his all-time record as the most successful driver in Mexico City Grand Prix history.
- **2024**: Carlos Sainz Jr. took pole position and a dominant victory for Scuderia Ferrari. Max Verstappen received two separate 10-second penalties for incidents with title contender Lando Norris.
