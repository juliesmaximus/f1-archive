# Circuit Profile: Circuito de Jerez

## Overview & Historic Legacy
The **Circuito de Jerez** (officially *Circuito de Jerez-Ángel Nieto*), located in the sun-drenched sherry region of Jerez de la Frontera in Andalusia, southern Spain, is one of the most famous test and racing circuits in modern Formula One history.

Opened in December 1985, Jerez hosted the **Spanish Grand Prix** for five consecutive seasons from **1986 to 1990**, as well as the **European Grand Prix** in **1994** and **1997**. Revered for its technical sequence of medium and fast sweeping curves, smooth surface, and sunny microclimate, Jerez was Formula One's primary winter testing sanctuary for three decades.

---

## Circuit Specifications
- **Location:** Jerez de la Frontera, Cádiz, Andalusia, Spain
- **Coordinates:** 36°42'30\"N, 6°02'03\"W
- **Original Length (1986):** 4.218 km (2.621 miles)
- **Modern Length:** 4.428 km (2.751 miles)
- **Turns:** 13 (modern layout)
- **Direction:** Clockwise
- **Key Turns:**
  - *Curva Expo '92 (Turn 1):* Heavy braking right-hander leading off the main straight.
  - *Curva Michelin & Curva Sito Pons:* High-speed flowing sweepers testing aerodynamic balance.
  - *Curva Dry Sack (Turn 6):* Sharp 90-degree right-hand hairpin at the end of the back straight.
  - *Curva Peluqui & Curva Nieto:* Fast technical right-hand complex surrounded by natural hillside grandstands.

## Monumental Formula One Moments
1. **1986 (The 0.014-Second Drag Race):** In the inaugural Spanish Grand Prix at Jerez, Ayrton Senna (Lotus-Renault) led on worn tires. Nigel Mansell (Williams-Honda) pitted for fresh rubber and tore back through a 20-second deficit at 3 seconds per lap! On the final lap, Mansell lunged inside at the final corner; they drag-raced down the straight side-by-side: Senna took victory by **0.014 seconds**—the third-closest finish in Formula One history!
2. **1990 (Martin Donnelly's Survival):** During Friday qualifying, Northern Irish driver Martin Donnelly suffered a 160 mph suspension failure at Curva Sito Pons. The Lotus 102 shattered on the Armco; Donnelly was thrown onto the asphalt still strapped to his carbon-fibre seat in a terrifying image of survival that galvanized neurosurgical safety reforms by Professor Sid Watkins.
3. **1997 (The Three-Way Qualifying Tie):** In qualifying for the 1997 European Grand Prix, an unprecedented statistical miracle occurred: **Jacques Villeneuve, Michael Schumacher, and Heinz-Harald Frentzen all set the exact same lap time to the thousandth of a second: 1:21.072!** Under FIA regulations, Villeneuve took pole because he had set the time first.
4. **1997 (The Dry Sack Clash & Title Decider):** On lap 48 of the title decider, Jacques Villeneuve dived down the inside of Michael Schumacher at the Dry Sack hairpin. Schumacher deliberately turned his Ferrari into the sidepod of Villeneuve's Williams. Schumacher was beached in the gravel trap, while Villeneuve continued with damaged bodywork to finish 3rd and claim the **1997 World Drivers' Championship**. The FIA subsequently disqualified Schumacher from the entire 1997 championship for unsporting conduct.

## Grand Prix Statistics & Records
- **Most Wins (Driver):** Alain Prost (2 victories: 1988, 1990); Ayrton Senna (2 victories: 1986, 1989); Nigel Mansell (1 victory: 1987); Michael Schumacher (1 victory: 1994); Mika Häkkinen (1 victory: 1997).
- **Most Wins (Constructor):** Williams (3 victories), McLaren (2 victories), Lotus (1 victory), Ferrari (1 victory).
- **Official F1 Race Lap Record:** 1:23.135 (Heinz-Harald Frentzen, Williams FW19, 1997).
