# Circuit Profile: Adelaide Street Circuit

## Overview & The Golden Era of Season Finales
The **Adelaide Street Circuit**, woven through the parklands and city boulevards of Adelaide's eastern suburbs in South Australia, is widely celebrated as one of the greatest temporary street circuits in motorsport history.

Serving as the traditional season finale of the FIA Formula One World Championship for eleven consecutive seasons from **1985 to 1995**, Adelaide set the global benchmark for circuit organization, driver safety, and spectator passion. The Australian Grand Prix in Adelaide was awarded the Formula One Promotional Trophy as the best-organized Grand Prix of the year on multiple occasions.

---

## Circuit Specifications
- **Location:** Adelaide Park Lands, Adelaide, South Australia
- **Coordinates:** 34°55'50\"S, 138°37'14\"E
- **Circuit Length:** 3.780 km (2.349 miles)
- **Turns:** 16
- **Direction:** Clockwise
- **Surface:** Smooth asphalt parkland roads and public thoroughfares
- **Key Features:**
  - *Senna Chicane (Turns 1, 2 & 3):* Aggressive right-left-right flick directly off the start/finish straight, flanked by high kerbs.
  - *Wakefield Road & East Terrace:* Fast 90-degree street junctions through leafy residential parklands.
  - *Brabham Straight:* Blistering 900-metre full-throttle blast down Jones Avenue, where 1,000+ bhp turbo machines reached 330 km/h (205 mph).
  - *Dequetteville Hairpin:* Heavy braking zone at the end of Brabham Straight, providing premier slipstream overtaking.

## Legendary Formula One Moments
1. **1985 Inaugural Australian GP:** Keke Rosberg won the ferocious inaugural race for Williams-Honda in sweltering 35°C heat, holding off the Ligiers of Laffite and Streiff.
2. **1986 Three-Way Championship Showdown:** One of the most famous championship finales in history:
   - Nigel Mansell entered needing only 3rd place to become champion. On lap 64 at 180 mph down Brabham Straight, Mansell's left-rear Goodyear tire violently exploded in a shower of sparks!
   - Williams pitted Nelson Piquet as a precaution, allowing Alain Prost in the McLaren MP4/2C to nurse his fuel home and snatch an improbable **second World Drivers' Championship**.
3. **1989 Monsoon Deluge:** Torrential tropical downpours created hazardous rivers across the track; Alain Prost voluntarily retired after one lap in protest of dangerous conditions, while Thierry Boutsen mastered the aquaplaning to win for Williams-Renault.
4. **1991 The Shortest Race in F1 History:** Struck by catastrophic monsoon storms, the race was stopped after just 14 laps (24 minutes and 34 seconds), with Ayrton Senna declared winner in the **shortest Grand Prix in Formula One history** (awarding half-points).
5. **1994 The Schumacher-Hill Title Decider:** Michael Schumacher (Benetton) damaged his car against the East Terrace wall on lap 36; as Damon Hill (Williams) attempted to pass at the next corner, Schumacher turned in, resulting in a controversial collision that eliminated both cars and handed Schumacher his **first World Drivers' Championship**.
6. **1995 Adelaide Farewell:** Hosted its final Grand Prix before moving to Melbourne; Mika Häkkinen survived a life-threatening qualifying crash, and Damon Hill won by two entire laps before a record crowd of 210,000 spectators.

## Grand Prix Statistics & Records
- **Most Wins (Driver):** Ayrton Senna (2 victories: 1991, 1993); Gerhard Berger (2 victories: 1987, 1992); Alain Prost (2 victories: 1986, 1988).
- **Most Wins (Constructor):** McLaren (5 victories: 1986, 1988, 1991, 1992, 1993), Williams (4 victories).
- **Official F1 Race Lap Record:** 1:15.381 (Damon Hill, Williams FW15C, 1993).
