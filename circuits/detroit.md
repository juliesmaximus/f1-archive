# Circuit Profile: Detroit Street Circuit

## Overview & Track History
The **Detroit Street Circuit** was a punishing, tight, barrier-lined street circuit set around the Renaissance Center and Cobo Hall along the Detroit River in downtown Detroit, Michigan, United States.

Known as the "Motor City", Detroit hosted the **Detroit Grand Prix** as a round of the FIA Formula One World Championship for seven consecutive seasons from **1982 to 1988**. Notorious for its brutal attrition, oppressive summer humidity, abrasive asphalt that disintegrated under race conditions, and narrow concrete channels, Detroit was one of the most physically demanding challenges on the Grand Prix calendar.

---

## Circuit Specifications
- **Location:** Downtown Detroit, Michigan, United States
- **Coordinates:** 42°19'48\"N, 83°02'24\"W
- **Circuit Type:** Temporary city street circuit
- **Length:** 4.023 km (2.500 miles)
- **Turns:** 17 (predominantly sharp 90-degree left and right street junctions)
- **Direction:** Counter-clockwise
- **Key Features:**
  - Fast blast down Jefferson Avenue towards the tunnel to Windsor, Ontario.
  - Demanding hairpin around the Renaissance Center towers.
  - Bumpy railway track crossings along Atwater Street that frequently jarred suspensions and caused steering failures.

## Memorable Formula One Moments
1. **1982 (Watson Wins from 17th):** McLaren's John Watson put on an overtaking clinic, carving through the entire field from 17th on the grid to score a sensational maiden Detroit victory.
2. **1983 (The DFV's Swan Song):** Michele Alboreto won for Tyrrell, scoring the **155th and final Formula One victory for the legendary Ford Cosworth DFV engine** that had debuted at Zandvoort in 1967.
3. **1984 (Piquet's Pole-to-Flag):** Nelson Piquet dominated in the Brabham-BMW turbo, surviving extreme attrition where only six cars were running at the finish.
4. **1985 (Keke Rosberg's Brawling Win):** Keke Rosberg muscled his 1,000 bhp Williams-Honda turbo through the heat, letting rivals overheat and crash to take a commanding victory.
5. **1986–1988 (Ayrton Senna's Hat-Trick):** Brazilian master Ayrton Senna turned Detroit into his personal domain, winning **three consecutive Detroit Grands Prix**:
   - 1986: Lotus 98T Renault turbo (won famously flying the Brazilian flag on his victory lap).
   - 1987: Lotus 99T Honda turbo (first win for active suspension in F1).
   - 1988: McLaren MP4/4 Honda turbo (dominating lights-to-flag by 38.7 seconds).

---

## Demise & Transition
By 1988, Formula One drivers and teams complained bitterly about the crumbling road surface, inadequate temporary garages, and harsh track bumps. Promoters and the city refused to fund a multimillion-dollar permanent pit complex demanded by Bernie Ecclestone, causing Formula One to relocate to Phoenix, Arizona for 1989. The Detroit Grand Prix transitioned to CART IndyCar, later moving to Belle Isle Park in 1992.
