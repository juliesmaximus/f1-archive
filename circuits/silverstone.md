# Silverstone Circuit — History & Statistics

**Silverstone Circuit**, straddling the Northamptonshire and Buckinghamshire border in the United Kingdom, is the historic birthplace of the FIA Formula One World Championship. Developed on the site of a former World War II Royal Air Force bomber station (RAF Silverstone), the circuit hosted the very first World Championship Grand Prix on 13 May 1950.

---

## Circuit Specifications

- **Location**: Silverstone, Northamptonshire, United Kingdom (52°04′43″N, 01°01′01″W)
- **Current Track Length**: 5.891 km (3.660 mi)
- **Turns**: 18 (Copse, Maggotts-Becketts-Chapel complex, Hangar Straight, Stowe, Vale, Club, Abbey, Farm, Village, The Loop, Aintree, Wellington Straight, Brooklands, Luffield, Woodcote)
- **Race Distance**: 52 laps (306.198 km / 190.263 mi)
- **Official Race Lap Record**: 
  - **1:27.097** — Max Verstappen, Red Bull Racing RB16 (2020 British Grand Prix, Lap 52).

  - **1:24.303** — Lewis Hamilton, Mercedes F1 W11 EQ Performance (2020 British Grand Prix, Q3 Pole Lap).
  - *Source*: FIA Official Qualifying Classification [2020 British GP](https://www.formula1.com/en/results.html/2020/races/1048/great-britain/qualifying.html)

## Landmark Milestones at Silverstone
- **1950 British Grand Prix (Round 1 of F1)**: Won by Giuseppe Farina driving the supercharged Alfa Romeo 158 "Alfetta" in front of King George VI.
- **1951 British Grand Prix**: José Froilán González scored Scuderia Ferrari's first-ever Formula One victory, breaking Alfa Romeo's dominance.
- **2020 70th Anniversary Grand Prix**: Hosted back-to-back with the British GP behind closed doors during the COVID-19 pandemic; won by Max Verstappen with superior tyre preservation.
- **Most Wins by Driver at Silverstone**: **Lewis Hamilton** holds the all-time record with **9 British Grand Prix victories** (2008, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2024), more than any driver at a single circuit in Formula 1 history.
