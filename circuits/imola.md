# Circuit Profile: Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari (Imola)

## Overview & Track History
The **Autodromo Internazionale Enzo e Dino Ferrari**, universally known as **Imola**, is one of the most hallowed, challenging, and emotionally charged racing circuits in motorsport history. Situated in the town of Imola in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, just 40 kilometers east of Scuderia Ferrari's headquarters in Maranello, Imola is considered Ferrari's spiritual home turf alongside Monza.

Unlike many modern purpose-built facilities, Imola is a fast, flowing, undulating natural-terrain circuit that runs counter-clockwise along the banks of the Santerno River.

## Championship Hosting History

### 1. The 1980 Italian Grand Prix
- Imola first hosted an official Formula One World Championship round in 1980, staging the **51st Italian Grand Prix** while the Autodromo Nazionale di Monza underwent extensive modernizations. Nelson Piquet won for Brabham-Ford.

### 2. The San Marino Grand Prix Era (1981–2006)
- Because Italian sporting regulations prohibited two Grands Prix titled "Italian Grand Prix" in the same championship season, Imola hosted the **San Marino Grand Prix** from 1981 through 2006, named after the nearby Republic of San Marino.
- During this 26-year tenure, Imola became the traditional opening European round of the Formula One calendar.

### 3. The Black Weekend of 1994
- The 1994 San Marino Grand Prix stands as the darkest weekend in modern Formula One history:
  - Friday, April 29: Rubens Barrichello survived a violent 140 mph crash at the Variante Bassa chicane.
  - Saturday, April 30: Austrian driver Roland Ratzenberger was killed when his Simtek front wing failed at 195 mph entering the Villeneuve chicane.
  - Sunday, May 1: Three-time World Champion Ayrton Senna suffered a fatal steering column failure at 190 mph entering the flat-out Tamburello curve, striking the concrete retaining wall at 135 mph.
- In response, the FIA and circuit management radically redesigned Imola for 1995: Tamburello and Villeneuve were transformed into tight chicanes, asphalt runoffs were added, and circuit safety standards worldwide were revolutionized.

### 4. Modern Return: Emilia Romagna Grand Prix (2020–Present)
- Returned to the Formula One calendar during the 2020 season under the banner of the **Gran Premio dell'Emilia Romagna**, reestablishing itself as a beloved fixture of the World Championship.

## Circuit Specifications
- **Location:** Imola, Emilia-Romagna, Italy
- **Coordinates:** 44°20'28"N, 11°42'49"E
- **Direction:** Counter-clockwise
- **Original Length (1980):** 5.000 km (3.107 miles)
- **Modern Length:** 4.909 km (3.050 miles)
- **Key Turns & Sectors:**
  - *Variante Tamburello:* High-speed left-right chicane replacing the original flat-out curve.
  - *Variante Villeneuve:* Rapid left-right chicane leading downhill.
  - *Curva Tosa:* Tight uphill hairpin offering prime overtaking opportunities and surrounded by steep natural amphitheatre banks.
  - *Piratella:* Tricky, blind downhill left-hander over a crest.
  - *Acque Minerali:* Complex, high-load double-apex right-hander dipping through the Santerno river basin.
  - *Variante Alta (Gresini Chicane):* Aggressive high-kerb chicane at the top of the hill.
  - *Rivazza 1 & 2:* Demanding downhill double left-handers onto the pit straight.

## Lap Records & Statistics
- **Most Wins (Driver):** Michael Schumacher (7 victories: 1994, 1999, 2000, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2006)
- **Most Wins (Constructor):** Scuderia Ferrari (8 victories) and Williams (8 victories)
- **Official F1 Lap Record:** 1:15.484 (Lewis Hamilton, Mercedes W11, 2020)
