## Team History & The Mid-Engine Revolution

Founded in 1946 by Charles Cooper and his son John Cooper in a modest garage in Surbiton, Surrey, the Cooper Car Company initiated the single greatest technological revolution in Formula One history: placing the engine behind the driver (rear mid-engine layout).

### Origins & Philosophy

The Coopers began by constructing 500cc Formula 3 racing cars powered by single-cylinder JAP motorcycle engines mounted behind the driver simply because it allowed a direct chain-drive to the rear axle. What started as mechanical expedience soon revealed overwhelming physical advantages:
- Dramatically reduced frontal surface area and aerodynamic drag.
- Elimination of the heavy, parasitic propeller driveshaft running beneath the driver's seat.
- Lower center of gravity and polar moment of inertia, enabling vastly superior cornering turn-in response and directional agility.
- Superior power-to-weight ratio and tire traction off slow-speed corners.

### The 1958 Breakthrough

In 1958, running the lightweight Cooper T43 and T45 chassis powered by 2.0-litre Coventry Climax 4-cylinder engines, Cooper proved that agile lightweight design could conquer brute horsepower:
- **1958 Argentine Grand Prix**: Stirling Moss drove Rob Walker's private Cooper T43-Climax to victory without a single tire stop, scoring the **FIRST-EVER WORLD CHAMPIONSHIP VICTORY FOR A REAR-ENGINE CAR**.
- **1958 Monaco Grand Prix**: Maurice Trintignant repeated the feat in Rob Walker's Cooper, dancing through the principality streets to win.

### 1959–1960 Championship Dominance

With the introduction of the full 2.5-litre Coventry Climax FPF engine in the factory Cooper T51 (designed by John Cooper and engineered by chief driver Jack Brabham):
- **1959**: Jack Brabham won the World Drivers' Championship, famously pushing his out-of-fuel car across the finish line at Sebring to finish 4th and clinch the title. Cooper swept the World Constructors' Championship (International Cup for F1 Manufacturers).
- **1960**: Brabham won five consecutive Grands Prix (Netherlands, Belgium, France, Britain, Portugal) in the Cooper T53 'Lowline' to win back-to-back World Championships, with Cooper securing their second consecutive Constructors' title.

### The Total Revolution

By 1961, the entire Formula One grid had surrendered to the Cooper philosophy. Ferrari, Lotus, BRM, and Porsche abandoned front-engine racing cars forever. The front-engine Formula One Grand Prix car became permanently extinct.

Cooper continued racing through the 1.5L and 3.0L eras with drivers such as Bruce McLaren, Jochen Rindt, and John Surtees (who scored Cooper's 16th and final victory at the 1967 Italian Grand Prix in the Cooper-Maserati T81B) before withdrawing from Formula One at the end of 1969.
