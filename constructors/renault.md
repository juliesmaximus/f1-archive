# Constructor Profile: Renault in Formula One

## Overview & Historic Legacy
Renault is one of the most transformative constructors and engine manufacturers in Formula One history. In 1977, Renault revolutionized Grand Prix motor racing by introducing forced induction with the first turbocharged engine in modern Formula One—a concept initially ridiculed as the "Yellow Teapot" due to early unreliability and steam clouds, but which ultimately forced every competitor to adopt turbocharging and reshaped the sport forever.

Renault achieved back-to-back FIA Formula One World Drivers' and Constructors' Championships as a full works team in 2005 and 2006 with Fernando Alonso, and powered world championship dynasties for Williams (1992, 1993, 1996, 1997), Benetton (1995), and Red Bull Racing (2010, 2011, 2012, 2013).

---

## Key Milestones

### 1. The Turbocharged Revolution (1977–1985)
- **1977 Debut:** At the 1977 British Grand Prix at Silverstone, Jean-Pierre Jabouille drove the works Renault RS01 powered by the 1.5-litre turbocharged V6 Gordini EF1 engine. Renault chose the 1.5-litre equivalence factor (permitted against 3.0-litre naturally aspirated engines since 1966 but never attempted in competition).
- **First Victory (1979 French GP, Dijon-Prenois):** Jean-Pierre Jabouille scored Renault's maiden victory on July 1, 1979, also marking the first Formula One World Championship victory for a turbocharged car.
- **Ground Effect & Power Escalation:** Under technical director Bernard Dudot, Renault pioneered twin-turbo setups and electronic engine management, prompting Ferrari, BMW, Porsche (TAG), and Honda to develop turbo engines.

### 2. The Engine Supplier Era (1989–1997)
- Following the ban on turbos after 1988, Renault developed the 3.5-litre naturally aspirated V10 (RS1 to RS9), establishing the benchmark engine configuration of the 1990s.
- Renault V10 engines powered Williams and Benetton to six consecutive Constructors' World Championships (1992–1997) and five Drivers' World Championships (Mansell 1992, Prost 1993, Schumacher 1995, Hill 1996, Villeneuve 1997).

### 3. The Works World Championship Era (2002–2006)
- Renault acquired the Enstone-based Benetton team in 2000, rebranding as Renault F1 Team in 2002 under Flavio Briatore, with chassis design led by Mike Gascoyne and Bob Bell.
- **2005:** Fernando Alonso won 7 races, securing both the World Drivers' and World Constructors' Championships with the Renault R25 (the final V10 champion).
- **2006:** Alonso and Giancarlo Fisichella retained both titles with the Renault R26 powered by the 2.4-litre RS26 V8 engine.

### 4. Red Bull V8 Dynasty & Turbo-Hybrid Return (2010–Present)
- Renault supplied Red Bull Racing with the RS27 V8 engine, winning four consecutive Drivers' and Constructors' World Championships from 2010 to 2013 with Sebastian Vettel.
- Works team returned in 2016, later rebranded to Alpine F1 Team in 2021, winning the 2021 Hungarian Grand Prix with Esteban Ocon.

## Key Technical Innovations
1. **1.5-Litre Turbocharging (1977):** Overcame extreme turbo lag through twin-turbo technology and wastegate development.
2. **Pneumatic Valve Return Springs (1986):** Replaced traditional metal springs with compressed nitrogen, allowing engine RPMs to exceed 14,000 and eventually 20,000 RPM.
3. **Mass Damper System (2005–2006):** Tuned mass damper located in the nosecone that stabilized front tire contact patch over kerbs.

- **First Win:** 1979 French Grand Prix (Jean-Pierre Jabouille, Renault RS10)
- **World Constructors' Championships:** 2 (2005, 2006)
- **World Drivers' Championships:** 2 (Fernando Alonso, 2005, 2006)
- **Total Grand Prix Wins (Works):** 36
- **Total Grand Prix Wins as Engine Manufacturer:** Over 175 wins
