## Team History & Engineering Legacy

Vanwall was created by British industrialist Guy Anthony 'Tony' Vandervell, founder of Vandervell Products (makers of the patented Thinwall bearing). Vandervell was a founding member of British Racing Motors (BRM), but became disillusioned with BRM's management and complex V16 engine. He resolved to build a world-class British Grand Prix car capable of beating Ferrari and Maserati.

### The Colin Chapman & Frank Costin Revolution

Vandervell recruited two visionary young engineers who transformed Formula One design:
1. **Colin Chapman** (founder of Lotus) was contracted to design a lightweight multi-tubular spaceframe chassis.
2. **Frank Costin** (de Havilland aerodynamicist) designed an aerodynamic, teardrop-shaped aluminum bodywork that enclosed the engine and radiator, giving the car unprecedented high-speed aerodynamic efficiency.

The car was powered by a 2,489cc 4-cylinder engine designed by Leo Kuzmicki, incorporating four 500cc Norton motorcycle cylinder barrels atop a Rolls-Royce B40 crankcase, fitted with advanced Bosch direct mechanical fuel injection and Goodyear aircraft disc brakes.

### Historic Breakthroughs

- **1957 British Grand Prix (Aintree)**: Tony Brooks and Stirling Moss shared car #20 to win the British Grand Prix, marking the **FIRST-EVER FORMULA ONE WORLD CHAMPIONSHIP VICTORY FOR A BRITISH CAR**, shattering the Italian continental stranglehold on Grand Prix racing.
- **1957 Season Finale (Monza)**: Vanwall scored a front-row qualifying sweep (Stuart Lewis-Evans, Stirling Moss, Tony Brooks) and Moss won on Italian soil at Monza.
- **1958 Inaugural Constructors' Championship**: In 1958, the FIA introduced the **International Cup for F1 Manufacturers** (World Constructors' Championship). Driven by Stirling Moss (4 wins) and Tony Brooks (3 wins), Vanwall won 6 of the 10 European rounds to become the **INAUGURAL FORMULA ONE WORLD CONSTRUCTORS' CHAMPIONS**, beating Scuderia Ferrari by 48 points to 40.

### Tragedy & Withdrawal

At the 1958 season finale in Casablanca, Morocco (the Moroccan Grand Prix), team driver Stuart Lewis-Evans suffered severe burns when his engine seized and crashed into flames. Lewis-Evans succumbed to his injuries six days later in an English hospital. Devastated by the loss of his protégé, Tony Vandervell announced the team's withdrawal from full-time racing at the end of 1958 due to failing health and grief. Vanwall made only sporadic non-competitive appearances in 1959 and 1960 before shuttering permanently.
