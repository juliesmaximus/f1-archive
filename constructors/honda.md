## Historical Significance

Founded by Soichiro Honda, Honda entered Formula One in 1964 as a complete works constructor, building both the chassis and the engine in-house—a feat only Ferrari matched at the time.

In the 1965 season finale at the high-altitude Magdalena Mixhuca circuit in Mexico City, American driver Richie Ginther drove the 1.5-litre transverse V12 Honda RA272 to victory, leading from start to finish. This achieved several historic firsts:
1. The first Formula One World Championship Grand Prix victory for a Japanese constructor.
2. The first Formula One victory for a Japanese car and engine.
3. The first Formula One World Championship victory for Goodyear tyres.

---

## Technical Innovation

- **Transverse V12 Engine (RA271 / RA272):** Drawing upon Honda's dominant Grand Prix motorcycle heritage, the RA272 featured a 1,495 cc 48-valve 60-degree V12 engine mounted transversely behind the driver, revving up to an unprecedented 13,000 rpm and producing over 230 bhp—far exceeding the power of British V8s.
- **The "Hondola" (RA300):** Developed in collaboration with Eric Broadley of Lola Cars, the RA300 won on its debut at Monza in 1967 with John Surtees.
