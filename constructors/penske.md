**Full Team Name:** Penske Cars Ltd. / Team Penske  
**Country:** United States  
**F1 Operational Base:** Poole, Dorset, England  
**Founder & Team Principal:** Roger Penske ("The Captain")  
**Chief Designer:** Geoff Ferris  
**Active Years as F1 Constructor:** 1974–1977  
**Grand Prix Starts:** 30  
**Wins:** 1 (1976 Austrian Grand Prix)  
**Podiums:** 3  
**Career Championship Points:** 23  

---

## Historical Significance

Founded by American motorsport titan Roger Penske, Team Penske holds an elite place in Formula One history. Penske remains the **last American-licensed constructor to win a Formula One World Championship Grand Prix**, taking victory at the 1976 Austrian Grand Prix with Northern Irish driver John Watson. Alongside Dan Gurney's Anglo American Racers (Eagle), Penske is one of only two American constructors ever to achieve a Grand Prix victory in the World Championship.

---

## Formula 1 History Overview

### Entry & Mark Donohue Tragedy (1974–1975)
- Debuted as a constructor at the 1974 Canadian Grand Prix with the Geoff Ferris-designed Penske PC1, driven by Penske's long-time friend and star driver Mark Donohue.
- In 1975, the team scored 5th in Sweden. Tragically, during Sunday morning warm-up for the 1975 Austrian Grand Prix at the Österreichring, Donohue suffered a tyre blowout at Vöest-Hügel and crashed heavily; he passed away two days later in hospital from a brain hemorrhage.

### The 1976 Triumph with John Watson
- Re-grouped for 1976 with Northern Irishman John Watson driving the new, streamlined **Penske PC4** powered by the Ford Cosworth DFV V8.
- Watson took consecutive 3rd-place podiums at Circuit Paul Ricard (France) and Brands Hatch (Britain).
- Exactly one year after Mark Donohue's fatal crash, Watson drove a transcendent race at the 1976 Austrian Grand Prix at the Österreichring, passing Jody Scheckter and pulling away to **win by 10.79 seconds**. In celebration, Watson famously shaved off his trademark beard.
- Team Penske finished 5th in the 1976 World Constructors' Championship with 20 points.

### Transition & Exit
- At the end of 1976, Roger Penske chose to refocus the organization's primary efforts on North American IndyCar racing, selling the F1 assets to German entrant Günter Schmid (forming the ATS team).
- Team Penske went on to become the most successful team in North American open-wheel history, winning a record 20 Indianapolis 500s.
