## Historical Significance

Founded by timber merchant and visionary talent scout Ken Tyrrell, the Tyrrell Racing Organisation was one of the most beloved and creatively inventive constructors in Formula One history.

- **Chassis Independence (1970–1973):** After running Matra and March customer chassis, Ken Tyrrell commissioned designer Derek Gardner in secret to construct the team's first proprietary car, the Tyrrell 001, debuting at Mont-Tremblant in 1970. In 1971, the iconic blue Tyrrell 003 Ford Cosworth DFV dominated the season, carrying Jackie Stewart to the Drivers' Championship and Tyrrell to the World Constructors' Championship. Stewart won a third title with Tyrrell in 1973.
- **The Six-Wheeled Tyrrell P34 (1976):** Designed by Derek Gardner, the radical Tyrrell P34 featured four small 10-inch wheels at the front to reduce frontal aerodynamic lift and increase front contact patch. In 1976, Jody Scheckter and Patrick Depailler scored a famous 1-2 finish at the Swedish Grand Prix at Anderstorp. The P34 remains the **only six-wheeled car to win a Formula One World Championship Grand Prix**.
- **The Raised Nose Revolution (Tyrrell 019, 1990):** Designed by Dr. Harvey Postlethwaite and Jean-Claude Migeot, the 1990 Tyrrell 019 introduced the revolutionary "anhedral" high nose, channeling clean airflow beneath the chassis to feed the rear diffuser. This design transformed racing aerodynamics and remains the fundamental aerodynamic architecture of every modern Formula One car today.
- **Heritage Legacy:** Sold at the end of 1998 to British American Tobacco to become BAR (British American Racing), the team later evolved into the factory Honda team, Brawn GP (2009 double World Champions), and eventually Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team.
