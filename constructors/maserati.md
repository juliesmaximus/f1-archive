# Officine Alfieri Maserati — History & Statistics

Founded in Bologna in 1914 by the Maserati brothers, Officine Alfieri Maserati (based in Modena, Italy) was one of the quintessential titans of post-war Grand Prix racing. Competing with the iconic 2.0-litre **A6GCM** and the legendary 2.5-litre straight-six **250F**, Maserati won the 1957 World Drivers' Championship with Juan Manuel Fangio and claimed 9 World Championship Grand Prix victories as a factory team before withdrawing at the end of 1957.

---

## Team Overview & Record

- **Official Historic Name**: Officine Alfieri Maserati
- **Headquarters**: Modena, Emilia-Romagna, Italy
- **License Nationality**: Italian
- **Key Engineering Figures**: Gioacchino Colombo, Vittorio Bellentani, Giulio Alfieri
- **World Drivers' Championships**: **1** (1957 — Juan Manuel Fangio)
- **World Constructors' Championships**: 0 (Withdrew as factory team prior to inaugural 1958 Constructors' Cup)
- **Total Grand Prix Starts**: 73 (as constructor across factory and privateer entries)
- **Total Grand Prix Wins**: **9** (First: Monza 1953; Final: Nürburgring 1957)
- **Total Pole Positions**: **10**
- **Total Podiums**: **37**
- **First World Championship Race**: 1950 British Grand Prix (Silverstone)
- **First World Championship Win**: 1953 Italian Grand Prix (Monza, 1953-09-13, Juan Manuel Fangio in Maserati A6GCM)

---

## The Legendary Maserati 250F
Introduced for the 1954 2.5-litre Formula, the Maserati 250F is widely considered one of the most beautiful and driver-friendly Grand Prix cars ever built:
- Engine: 2,493 cc naturally aspirated DOHC inline-6 (240 to 270 bhp)
- Chassis: Multi-tubular spaceframe with De Dion rear axle and inboard drum/disc brakes
- Drivers: Juan Manuel Fangio, Stirling Moss, Jean Behra, Harry Schell, Carroll Shelby, Jo Bonnier, Maria Teresa de Filippis (first woman in F1).
