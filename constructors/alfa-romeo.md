# Alfa Romeo in Formula One — History & Statistics

Alfa Romeo was the dominant force in Grand Prix racing leading into and through the creation of the FIA Formula One World Championship, winning the first two World Drivers' Championships in 1950 (with Giuseppe Farina) and 1951 (with Juan Manuel Fangio). Their pre-war designed Alfa Romeo 158 and 159 "Alfetta" (1.5-litre supercharged straight-8, producing up to 425 bhp at 9,300 RPM) won all 6 European championship Grands Prix in 1950.

---

## Team Overview & Record

- **Official Historic Name**: Alfa Romeo SpA
- **Headquarters**: Portello, Milan, Italy
- **License Nationality**: Italian
- **Team Leadership**: Antonio Alessio (General Manager), Gioacchino Colombo (Chief Engineer)
- **World Drivers' Championships**: **2** (1950 Farina, 1951 Fangio)
- **World Constructors' Championships**: 0 (The International Cup for F1 Manufacturers was introduced in 1958, after Alfa Romeo's factory withdrawal)
- **Total Grand Prix Starts**: 214 (as constructor / entrant across 1950–1951, 1979–1985, and Sauber title partnership 2019–2023)
- **Total Grand Prix Wins**: **10** (6 in 1950, 4 in 1951)
- **Total Pole Positions**: **12**
- **Total Podiums**: **26**
- **First Race**: 1950 British Grand Prix (Silverstone, 1950-05-13, Farina won from pole)
- **Final Grand Prix Win**: 1951 Spanish Grand Prix (Pedralbes, 1951-10-28, Juan Manuel Fangio)

---

## The Legendary 158/159 "Alfetta"
- Engine: 1,479 cc Roots-supercharged inline-8
- Power Output: 350 bhp (1950 158) to 425 bhp (1951 159)
- Weight: ~710 kg
- Record: Undefeated in the 1950 World Championship season (6 wins out of 6 European races entered).
