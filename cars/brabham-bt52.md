# Historic Car Profile: Brabham BT52 (1983)

## Overview & Historic Legacy
The **Brabham BT52** is one of the most visually distinctive, aerodynamically radical, and historically momentous racing cars in the history of Formula One. Conceived and built in just six weeks by Brabham technical director **Gordon Murray** following FISA's sudden ban on ground-effect aerodynamics in late 1982, the dart-shaped BT52 propelled **Nelson Piquet** to the 1983 FIA Formula One World Drivers' Championship—making it the **first turbocharged car in Formula One history to win the World Championship**.

Powered by BMW's legendary 1.5-litre M12/13 turbocharged inline-four engine producing upwards of 850 bhp in race trim and over 1,000 bhp in qualifying, the BT52 pioneered the planned pit stop revolution and redefined Grand Prix chassis architecture.

---

## Technical Innovation & Design Architecture

### 1. The Dart Silhouette & Weight Distribution
- When FISA mandated flat bottoms between the wheels for 1983, conventional long sidepods became aerodynamic dead weight that generated no underbody downforce.
- Gordon Murray radically scrapped his ground-effect BT51 design and conceived an aggressive arrow-shaped dart. The front of the car featured no sidepods at all; all radiators, oil coolers, and intercoolers were condensed into tiny triangular sidepods positioned far back alongside the engine.
- This shifted roughly **70% of the car's static weight onto the rear wheels**, recovering the mechanical rear traction lost from the ban on sliding skirts.

### 2. The Pit Stop & Refuelling Strategy
- Murray realized that running half-sized fuel tanks allowed the car to start roughly 100 kg lighter, qualifying higher and pulling away on softer-compound Michelin tires.
- Brabham engineered pressurized fuel rigs and high-speed air jacks, performing routine 10-to-12-second refuelling and tire stops that transformed Grand Prix race strategy from endurance preservation into flat-out sprint heats.

## Technical Specifications
- **Chassis:** Carbon-fibre and aluminium honeycomb composite monocoque
- **Engine:** BMW M12/13, 1,499 cc (1.5 L), turbocharged inline-4 cylinder
- **Engine Block:** Standard cast-iron BMW M10 road-car block seasoned outdoors
- **Turbocharger:** Single KKK turbocharger with air-to-air intercooler
- **Power Output:** 740–850 bhp @ 10,500 RPM (Race); ~1,000+ bhp (Qualifying at 4.0+ bar boost)
- **Transmission:** Brabham/Hewland 6-speed manual
- **Fuel:** Custom synthetic hydrocarbon fuel blend developed by BASF/Wintershall
- **Tires:** Michelin radial tires
