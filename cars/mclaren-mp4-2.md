# Historic Car Profile: McLaren MP4/2 (1984–1986)

## Overview & Three-Year Dynasty
The **McLaren MP4/2** (alongside its evolutions, the **MP4/2B** of 1985 and **MP4/2C** of 1986) is one of the most relentlessly successful and dominant racing car designs in the history of the FIA Formula One World Championship. Designed by technical genius **John Barnard**, the MP4/2 series delivered **three consecutive World Drivers' Championships** (Niki Lauda in 1984, Alain Prost in 1985 and 1986) and **two World Constructors' Championships** (1984, 1985), scoring 22 Grand Prix victories across 48 starts.

Financed by Mansour Ojjeh's Techniques d'Avant Garde (TAG) and powered by an extraordinary 1.5-litre twin-turbocharged V6 engineered by Hans Mezger at Porsche, the MP4/2 set the gold standard for aerodynamic packaging, fuel management, and carbon-composite construction.

---

## Design Innovations & Technical Mastery

### 1. The Coke-Bottle Sculpted Rear
- John Barnard pioneered the distinctive "coke-bottle" rear end: the sidepods narrowed progressively towards the rear of the car, tapering between the rear wheels.
- This design accelerated airflow around the rear suspension and over the rear diffuser, extracting enormous levels of aerodynamic downforce under the newly mandated flat-bottom regulations without incurring excessive drag.

### 2. The TAG-Porsche Twin-Turbo V6 (TTE PO1)
- When Ron Dennis sought a bespoke turbo engine, he secured funding from TAG to commission **Porsche** to design and build an exclusive Grand Prix engine.
- Engineered by legendary Porsche racing engine designer **Hans Mezger**, the 1.5-litre 90-degree V6 utilized twin KKK turbochargers and Bosch Motronic electronic engine management.
- The engine was specifically designed for narrow integration into Barnard's chassis, optimizing center of gravity and aerodynamic airflow rather than pursuing peak qualifying power alone.
- In 1984, with mid-race refuelling banned and fuel capped at 220 litres, the TAG-Porsche engine achieved unrivaled thermal efficiency and computer-controlled fuel economy, allowing Lauda and Prost to maintain flat-out race pace while rivals ran dry.

## Technical Specifications (1984 MP4/2)
- **Chassis:** Moulded carbon-fibre composite monocoque with honeycomb core
- **Engine:** TAG-Porsche TTE PO1, 1,499 cc (1.5 L), 90° V6, twin-turbocharged
- **Turbochargers:** Twin KKK turbochargers with twin Behr intercoolers
- **Power Output:** 750–800 bhp @ 11,200 RPM (1984 race); ~850 bhp (1985); ~960 bhp (1986 qualifying)
- **Transmission:** McLaren/Hewland 5-speed manual (later 6-speed)
- **Weight:** 540 kg (meeting minimum weight limit)
- **Brakes:** Carbon-on-carbon brake discs and pads (SEP Carbone Industrie)
- **Tires:** Michelin radial tires (1984), Goodyear (1985–1986)

## Championship Summary
- **1984 (MP4/2):** 12 wins in 16 races (Prost 7, Lauda 5). Constructors' Champion (143.5 pts). Drivers' Champion: Niki Lauda (72 pts) by 0.5 points over Prost (71.5 pts).
- **1985 (MP4/2B):** 6 wins in 16 races (Prost 5, Lauda 1). Constructors' Champion (90 pts). Drivers' Champion: Alain Prost (73 pts - France's first World Champion).
- **1986 (MP4/2C):** 4 wins in 16 races (all Alain Prost). Drivers' Champion: Alain Prost (72 pts - first back-to-back champion since Jack Brabham in 1960).
