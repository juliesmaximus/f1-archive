# Ferrari 640 - Technical Dossier & Racing Record

## Overview
- **Team:** Scuderia Ferrari SpA SEFAC
- **Designer:** John Barnard (Technical Director)
- **Engine:** Ferrari Tipo 035/5, 3,498 cc (3.5 L) 65° V12 naturally aspirated (mid-mounted)
- **Power Output:** ~660 bhp @ 13,800 rpm
- **Chassis:** Carbon-fibre and Kevlar honeycomb monocoque
- **Transmission:** Ferrari 7-speed longitudinal semi-automatic sequential with electro-hydraulic paddle shifters
- **Weight:** 503 kg
- **Tyres:** Goodyear

---

## Technical Innovation: The Semi-Automatic Gearbox
The Ferrari 640 represents one of the greatest technological breakthroughs in Formula 1 history. Conceived by technical director John Barnard, it was the first racing car to replace the traditional mechanical manual gear lever and foot clutch with electro-hydraulic steering wheel-mounted paddle shifters.
Key advantages included:
1. **Uninterrupted Grip:** Drivers never had to remove their hands from the steering wheel while cornering or shifting.
2. **Cockpit Narrowing:** Eliminating the gearshift linkage allowed John Barnard to design an ultra-narrow, aerodynamic cockpit with sidepod air intakes shaped like jet engine inlets.
3. **Engine Over-rev Protection:** Electronic solenoid actuators prevented downshift over-revving.

Although early reliability suffered as Ferrari perfected the electrical solenoid wiring, Nigel Mansell won on the car's racing debut at the 1989 Brazilian Grand Prix, and Gerhard Berger won at Estoril. The paddle-shift system revolutionized Grand Prix racing and was eventually adopted by every team on the grid and across world motorsport.
