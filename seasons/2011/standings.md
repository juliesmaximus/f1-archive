## 3. Season Highlights & Historical Significance
- **Sebastian Vettel Youngest Double World Champion:** At 24 years, 98 days, Sebastian Vettel won his second consecutive World Drivers' Championship, clinching the title at Suzuka with four rounds remaining. Vettel set an all-time Formula 1 record of 15 pole positions in a single season (breaking Nigel Mansell's 1992 record of 14) and claimed 11 victories and 17 podium finishes.
- **Red Bull Racing Consecutive Constructors' Crown:** Red Bull Racing dominated the championship, scoring 650 points with 12 race victories and 18 pole positions out of 19 rounds.
- **Introduction of DRS & Pirelli Control Tyres:** The Drag Reduction System (DRS) made its debut alongside high-degradation Pirelli P Zero tires, dramatically multiplying overtaking maneuvers across the season.
- **Jenson Button's Epic 4-Hour Canadian GP Victory:** The 2011 Canadian Grand Prix entered the record books as the longest race in Formula 1 history (4 hours, 4 minutes, 39 seconds). Despite six pit stops, a collision with teammate Hamilton, a drive-through penalty, and dropping to 21st (last) place, Jenson Button overtook Sebastian Vettel on the final lap to take victory.
- **Inaugural Indian Grand Prix:** Formula 1 staged its first Grand Prix in India at the Hermann Tilke-designed Buddh International Circuit in Greater Noida, won by Sebastian Vettel who achieved the Grand Chelem (pole, win, fastest lap, led every lap).
