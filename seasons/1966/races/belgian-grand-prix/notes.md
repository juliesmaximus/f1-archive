- **Weather & Track Conditions:** Dry start, then a catastrophic torrential downpour, hail, and flash flooding on lap 1 at the far end of the circuit (Burnenville to Masta)  
- **Spectator Attendance:** 55,000 spectators in the Ardennes pine forests  
- **Grandstand & Infrastructure:** Grandstand opposite pits, Burnenville embankments, and Blanchimont slopes  

### Race Anomalies, Incidents & Key Events
One of the most consequential races in F1 safety history. The grid started on a dry pit straight, but on lap 1 drivers were met by a wall of torrential rain and hail between Burnenville and the Masta Kink. Seven cars spun off or crashed on lap 1 (Bonnier, Spence, Siffert, Hulme, Rindt, Hill, and Stewart). Jackie Stewart aquaplaned off at 150 mph into a ditch, hitting a woodcutter's cottage and a telegraph pole, ending upside down trapped in his cockpit, pinned by broken steering column with ruptured fuel tanks soaking him in high-octane aviation fuel. Teammate Graham Hill and Bob Bondurant stopped to rescue Stewart, using borrowed spectator spanners to dismantle the steering wheel. This terrifying ordeal inspired Stewart's legendary crusade for medical and circuit safety. John Surtees won for Ferrari ahead of Jochen Rindt (Cooper-Maserati). Only 5 cars finished the race.

### Historical & Regulatory Context
Last race for John Surtees with Scuderia Ferrari. Following a bitter dispute with team manager Eugenio Dragoni during Le Mans preparations, Surtees walked out of the team while leading the World Championship.
