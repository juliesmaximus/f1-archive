# 1955 Round 7: XXVI Gran Premio d'Italia - Verified Race Record

## Circuit & Geographic Metadata
- **Official Race Title**: XXVI Gran Premio d'Italia
- **Historic Name Notes**: 26th Italian Grand Prix; debut of Monza's high-speed 10.000 km combined road-and-banked-oval circuit; final race of the 1955 season.
- **Circuit Full Name**: Autodromo Nazionale di Monza (Pista Combinata)
- **Location**: Monza, Lombardy, Italy
- **Host Country**: Italy
- **Track Length**: 10.000 km (6.214 mi)
- **Track Altitude**: 162 m (531 ft) above sea level
- **Race Distance**: 50 laps / 500.000 km (310.686 mi)
- **Official Race Date**: 1955-09-11

## Weather, Environment & Track Conditions
- **Weather & Track Conditions**: Sunny, dry, warm autumn afternoon on the high-speed concrete banking.

- **Pole Position**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Mercedes-Benz W196 Streamliner) – 2:46.500
- **Fastest Race Lap**: Stirling Moss (Mercedes-Benz W196) – 2:46.900 (Lap 21) [1 pt]
- **Race Winner**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Mercedes-Benz W196 Streamliner) – 2:25:04.400 (8 pts)

## Verified Race Scenarios, Anomalies & Controversies
- **Monza High-Speed Banked Oval Debut**: The new 10.000 km combined track incorporated the 38-degree reinforced concrete banking (*sopraelevata*), generating brutal centrifugal forces and tyre destruction at speeds over 280 km/h.
- **Fangio Clinches 3rd World Championship**: Driving the closed-wheel streamlined W196, Fangio won the race at an average speed of 206.792 km/h, securing his THIRD WORLD DRIVERS' CHAMPIONSHIP.
- **Mercedes-Benz Factory Farewell**: At the post-race celebration, Daimler-Benz officially announced their complete withdrawal from all motorsport competition, closing their triumphant 1954–1955 era with back-to-back world drivers' championships. Mercedes would not return as a factory team until 2010.
