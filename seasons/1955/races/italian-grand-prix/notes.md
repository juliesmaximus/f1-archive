# 1955 Round 7: XXVI Gran Premio d'Italia - Verified Race Record

## Circuit & Geographic Metadata
- **Official Race Title**: XXVI Gran Premio d'Italia
- **Historic Name Notes**: 26th Italian Grand Prix; inaugural race on the new 10.000 km combined road and high-speed banked oval track.
- **Circuit Full Name**: Autodromo Nazionale di Monza (10 km combined)
- **Location**: Monza, Lombardy, Italy
- **Host Country**: Italy
- **Track Length**: 10.000 km (6.214 mi)
- **Track Altitude**: 162 m (531 ft) above sea level
- **Race Distance**: 50 laps / 500.000 km (310.686 mi)
- **Official Race Date**: 1955-09-11

## Weather, Environment & Spectators
- **Weather & Track Conditions**: Warm, sunny, dry autumn conditions.
- **Spectator Attendance**: Over 150,000 spectators.
- **Grandstand & Infrastructure Notes**: New concrete high-speed banking (sopraelevata) grandstands and infield banking mounds packed.

## Verified Race Scenarios, Anomalies & Controversies
- Monza High-Speed Oval Debut: Monza constructed massive reinforced concrete banking (*sopraelevata*), combining the road course and oval into a breathtaking 10.000 km circuit. Cars attained speeds exceeding 280 km/h on the steep 38-degree banking.
- Severe Suspension and Tyre Punishment: The immense G-forces and bumpy concrete joints destroyed tyres and snapped suspensions. Ferrari withdrew their cars during practice due to tyre tread separations before Fiat/Lancia intervened.
- Fangio Clinches 3rd World Championship: Fangio won in the streamlined W196 at a record average speed of 206.792 km/h, clinching his 3rd World Drivers' Championship.
- Mercedes-Benz Motorsport Farewell: At the post-race banquet, Daimler-Benz officially announced their withdrawal from all factory motorsport activities at the end of the 1955 season, bowing out at the absolute peak of success.

## Records Set or Equalled
- Juan Manuel Fangio crowned 1955 Formula One World Champion of Drivers (3rd title).
- Fastest World Championship Grand Prix average race speed to date: 206.792 km/h (Fangio).
- Final World Championship race for Mercedes-Benz as a factory constructor until 2010.
