# 1957 German Grand Prix - Race Notes

## Official Race Identification
- **Official Name**: XIX Großer Preis von Deutschland
- **Round**: 6 of 8 in the 1957 FIA Formula One World Championship
- **Date**: Sunday, 4 August 1957
- **Location**: Nürburgring Nordschleife, Nürburg, Germany
- **Circuit Length**: 22.810 km (14.173 miles)
- **Distance**: 22 laps (501.820 km / 311.816 miles)

## Official Results & Key Metrics
- **Pole Position**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Maserati 250F) - 9:25.600
- **Fastest Lap**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Maserati 250F) - 9:17.400 (Lap 20) [1 pt]
- **Race Winner**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Maserati 250F) - 3:30:38.300 (142.924 km/h)
- **Podium**:
  1. Juan Manuel Fangio (Maserati) - 22 laps
  2. Mike Hawthorn (Ferrari) - 22 laps (+3.600s)
  3. Peter Collins (Ferrari) - 22 laps (+35.600s)

## Verified Race Scenarios & Primary-Source Facts
- **Fangio's Greatest Race / Fifth World Championship Clinch**: Widely regarded as one of the greatest individual driving performances in motorsport history. Fangio started on pole with softer Pirelli tires and half fuel tank, planning a scheduled pit stop. He opened a 30-second lead over the non-stopping Ferraris of Hawthorn and Collins.
- **The Catastrophic Pit Stop**: On lap 12, Fangio pitted. A rear wheel nut rolled under the car, resulting in a disastrous 52-second stop. Fangio rejoined 48 seconds behind Hawthorn and Collins with only 10 laps remaining.
- **The Charge of the Century**: Fangio unleashed an extraordinary sequence of record-shattering laps, breaking the circuit lap record 9 times in 10 laps and taking 6 to 8 seconds per lap out of the Ferrari lead. He lowered the lap record to an astonishing 9:17.400 (8.2 seconds faster than his pole time).
- **The Penultimate Lap Overtakes**: On lap 21, Fangio caught Collins on the straight before the North Curve, passing him into the corner with two wheels on the grass. Later in the same lap, he slipped inside Hawthorn at the Adenau bridge left-hander to retake the lead. Fangio crossed the line 3.6 seconds ahead of Hawthorn to seal his 24th and final career World Championship Grand Prix victory and secure his fifth World Championship title.
- **Track & Weather Conditions**: Overcast, cool Eifel mountain weather with dry track.
