## Historical Context & Narrative Summary
The 1991 Belgian Grand Prix is immortalized as the Formula 1 debut of Michael Schumacher. With regular Jordan driver Bertrand Gachot imprisoned in London following an altercation with a taxi driver, team owner Eddie Jordan signed 22-year-old German rookie Michael Schumacher on the recommendation of Mercedes-Benz. Having never driven Spa in an F1 car, Schumacher stunned the paddock by qualifying 7th on the grid, matching Jordan's best qualifying result of the season.

In the race, Schumacher made a brilliant start up to 5th, but burned out his clutch entering Eau Rouge on the opening lap. Nigel Mansell took the lead for Williams and seemed destined for victory until his electrical system failed on lap 22. Andrea de Cesaris in the sister Jordan 191 mounted an astonishing charge, running 2nd and closing rapidly on Senna until his Ford engine expired with three laps remaining. Senna survived to lead a McLaren 1-2 with Berger.

---

## The Contract Drama
Schumacher's sensational debut triggered immediate legal warfare. Benetton boss Flavio Briatore moved swiftly to sign Schumacher for the remainder of the season, unceremoniously replacing Roberto Moreno despite legal injunctions filed by Jordan.
