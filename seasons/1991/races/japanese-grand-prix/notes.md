## Historical Context & Narrative Summary
The championship-deciding showdown at Suzuka. Nigel Mansell needed to win to prevent Ayrton Senna from clinching the World Championship. McLaren locked out the front row with Gerhard Berger on pole ahead of Senna.

At the start, Berger led Senna, while Mansell ran 3rd. Mansell pushed aggressively to catch the McLarens, but on lap 10, entering the First Curve, Mansell suffered a front brake lockup, slid wide into the gravel trap, and was eliminated from the race. With Mansell out, Ayrton Senna was officially crowned 1991 World Drivers' Champion, capturing his 3rd World Championship title.

Senna and Berger dominated the remainder of the race. On the final lap, Senna led Berger out of the Casino Chicane towards the finish line. In a famous gesture of sportsmanship and gratitude for Berger's unwavering teamwork throughout the year, Senna lifted off the throttle on the pit straight and waved Berger past to take victory by 0.344 seconds.
