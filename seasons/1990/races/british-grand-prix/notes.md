## Historical Context & Narrative Summary
Nigel Mansell delighted his home crowd by securing pole position with a lap of 1:07.428 and dominated the race, trading the lead with Ayrton Senna and Gerhard Berger. Mansell pulled away and set fastest lap on lap 51, looking set for a triumphant home victory until his Ferrari 641's semi-automatic gearbox failed on lap 55.

A heartbroken Mansell parked his Ferrari at Club corner, threw his racing gloves into the cheering crowd, and announced his retirement from Formula 1 in post-race press conferences (a decision he later reversed when Frank Williams signed him for 1991). Alain Prost inherited the lead to score his third consecutive victory, taking the lead in the World Drivers' Championship.
