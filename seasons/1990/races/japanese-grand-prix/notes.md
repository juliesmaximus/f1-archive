## Historical Context & Narrative Summary
The most infamous title-deciding collision in Formula 1 history. Ayrton Senna captured pole position ahead of Alain Prost, but requested that pole be moved to the clean left side of the track. When FISA president Jean-Marie Balestre and the race stewards rejected the request, Senna resolved that if Prost made a better start from the cleaner line, he would not yield into Turn 1.

At the green light, Prost made a superior launch and took the lead. Entering Turn 1 at 160 mph, Senna kept his foot flat to the floor and deliberately rammed the right rear of Prost's Ferrari. Both cars slid into the gravel trap and retired on the spot. With Prost eliminated, Senna clinched his 2nd World Drivers' Championship. Senna openly confessed at Suzuka in 1991 that the collision was intentional in protest against FISA bias.

With the championship leaders out, Nelson Piquet and Roberto Moreno took an emotional 1-2 victory for Benetton. Roberto Moreno, who had started the year struggling to pre-qualify with EuroBrun, wept openly on the podium alongside his childhood friend and mentor Piquet. Aguri Suzuki finished 3rd in the Larrousse-entered Lola-Lamborghini, becoming the first Japanese driver in Formula 1 history to stand on a podium.
