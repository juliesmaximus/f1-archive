# Formula 1 World Championship Season 1980 - Final Standings

## World Drivers' Championship

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis to the top six finishers at each Grand Prix. Under the split-season dropped-score system, only the best **5** results from the first 7 races and the best **5** results from the final 7 races counted towards the final Drivers' Championship classification.

Alan Jones won the 1980 FIA Formula One World Drivers' Championship driving the Williams FW07B Ford Cosworth DFV, scoring 67 net points (71 gross) with 5 victories. Nelson Piquet (Brabham) finished as runner-up with 54 net points.

## International Cup for F1 Manufacturers (Constructors' Championship)

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis for all top six finishers of each constructor at each Grand Prix. All results counted towards the Constructors' Championship (no dropped scores), and both cars of a constructor could score points.

Williams Grand Prix Engineering captured their first-ever World Constructors' Championship with a commanding 120 points, winning 6 Grands Prix with the Williams FW07B. Equipe Ligier finished runner-up with 66 points.

## Regulatory Notes & Scoring System

- **Scoring System:** 9-6-4-3-2-1 for top 6 finishers.
- **Drivers' Dropped Scores Rule:** Split scoring: best 5 results from rounds 1–7 and best 5 results from rounds 8–14 counted towards the Drivers' Championship.
- **Constructors' Scoring Rule:** All scores counted towards the Constructors' Championship (no dropped rounds), and both cars of each constructor were eligible to score points.
- **FISA-FOCA War & The Spanish GP:** The Spanish Grand Prix held on June 1 at Jarama was declared non-championship following the FISA-FOCA conflict over sliding skirts; official records treat 1980 as a 14-race championship.
- **Historic Milestones:**
  - Alan Jones became the first Australian World Champion since Sir Jack Brabham in 1966.
  - Williams Grand Prix Engineering won their maiden World Constructors' Championship.
  - Nelson Piquet and Didier Pironi both scored their maiden Grand Prix victories (Long Beach and Zolder, respectively).
  - Clay Regazzoni suffered a career-ending crash at Long Beach due to a broken brake pedal.
  - Patrick Depailler tragically lost his life in a testing crash at Hockenheim in August.
  - The Italian Grand Prix was held at Imola for the only time in World Championship history.
  - Watkins Glen hosted its 20th and final Grand Prix.
  - Alain Prost made his World Championship debut for McLaren, scoring a point on debut in Argentina.
  - Nigel Mansell made his World Championship debut for Lotus at the Austrian GP.
