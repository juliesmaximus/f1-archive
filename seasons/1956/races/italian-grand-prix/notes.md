# 1956 Italian Grand Prix - Race Notes

## Official Race Identification
- **Official Name**: XXVII Gran Premio d'Italia
- **Round**: 8 of 8 in the 1956 FIA Formula One World Championship
- **Date**: Sunday, 2 September 1956
- **Location**: Autodromo Nazionale di Monza (Combined 10 km Road + High-Speed Oval Track), Monza, Italy
- **Circuit Length**: 10.000 km (6.214 miles)
- **Distance**: 50 laps (500.000 km / 310.686 miles)

## Official Results & Key Metrics
- **Pole Position**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Ferrari D50) - 2:42.600
- **Fastest Lap**: Stirling Moss (Maserati 250F) - 2:45.500 (Lap 47) [1 pt]
- **Race Winner**: Stirling Moss (Maserati 250F) - 2:23:41.300 (208.785 km/h)
- **Podium**:
  1. Stirling Moss (Maserati) - 50 laps
  2. Peter Collins & Juan Manuel Fangio (Ferrari) - 50 laps (+5.700s)
  3. Ron Flockhart (Connaught-Alta) - 49 laps (+1 lap)

## Verified Race Scenarios & Primary-Source Facts
- **Peter Collins' Legendary Sportsmanship**: On lap 30, Fangio's Ferrari suffered broken steering and he parked in the pits. Ferrari teammate Luigi Musso refused to hand over his car. On lap 35, Peter Collins pitted for routine tyre service. Knowing that by doing so he was forfeiting his own mathematical chance to win the World Drivers' Championship, Collins unhesitatingly handed his Ferrari #26 over to Fangio, saying Fangio deserved to win the title. Fangio drove the car home to 2nd place, securing 3 points and clinching his fourth World Championship.
- **Moss's Monza Victory & Fuel Scare**: Stirling Moss drove a brilliant race in his Maserati 250F. With a few laps remaining, his car ran out of fuel on the high-speed banking. Teammate Luigi Piotti nudged Moss's car with the nose of his Maserati 250F all the way back to the pits, allowing Moss to refuel and rejoin just ahead of Fangio to take the victory.
- **Connaught's First Championship Podium**: Scottish driver Ron Flockhart drove an outstanding race in the British Connaught Type B to finish 3rd, scoring the first World Championship podium for Connaught.
- **Tire Shredding on the High Banking**: The combined 10 km circuit, featuring the 30-degree banked concrete oval, subjected tyres to extreme centrifugal forces. Multiple cars, including the Ferraris of Castellotti and de Portago, suffered violent tread delaminations and suspension failures.
- **Track & Weather Conditions**: Very warm, bright late-summer sunshine; fast, dry track.
