## Verified Race Scenarios & Primary-Source Facts
- **Formula One's Darkest Weekend**: The 1960 Belgian Grand Prix weekend at the fearsome 14.1 km Spa circuit remains one of the darkest and deadliest in Grand Prix history:
  - During Friday practice, Stirling Moss suffered a broken rear axle hub at 140 mph at Burnenville, crashed heavily, and suffered broken legs, ribs, and back injuries. Mike Taylor suffered a steering failure in his Lotus, crashed into trees, and suffered paralyzing injuries.
  - During Sunday's race, British driver Chris Bristow lost control of his Cooper at Burnenville on lap 19 and was killed instantly when hurled into barbed wire.
  - Just five laps later on lap 24, Team Lotus driver Alan Stacey was struck in the face by a bird near the 140-mph Masta Kink, crashed into the woods, and died in the ensuing fire.
- **Cooper 1-2-3 Sweep**: Unaware of the fatalities unfolding behind him, Jack Brabham led every lap from pole to lead a dominant Cooper 1-2-3 finish with Bruce McLaren and Olivier Gendebien.
- **Track & Weather Conditions**: Warm, dry Ardennes afternoon with clear skies.
