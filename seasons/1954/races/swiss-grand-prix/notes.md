# 1954 Round 7: XIV Großer Preis der Schweiz / XIV Grand Prix de Suisse - Verified Race Record

## Circuit & Geographic Metadata
- **Official Race Title**: XIV Großer Preis der Schweiz / XIV Grand Prix de Suisse
- **Historic Name Notes**: 14th Swiss Grand Prix; title clincher for Fangio; the FINAL Swiss Grand Prix in Formula One history.
- **Circuit Full Name**: Circuit Bremgarten
- **Location**: Bremgarten bei Bern, Canton of Bern, Switzerland
- **Host Country**: Switzerland
- **Track Length**: 7.280 km (4.524 mi)
- **Track Altitude**: 550 m (1804 ft) above sea level
- **Race Distance**: 66 laps / 480.480 km (298.556 mi)
- **Official Race Date**: 1954-08-22

## Weather, Environment & Spectators
- **Weather & Track Conditions**: Rain at start, wet and greasy tree-lined track, drying late in the race.
- **Spectator Attendance**: Estimated 70,000 spectators.
- **Grandstand & Infrastructure Notes**: Final appearance of the historic Bremgarten wooden pit grandstand.

## Verified Race Scenarios, Anomalies & Controversies
- Fangio Clinches 2nd World Championship: Juan Manuel Fangio drove a commanding race in the wet, winning by nearly a minute over José Froilán González to mathematically clinch the 1954 FIA World Drivers' Championship with two races remaining.
- Title with Two Constructors: Fangio achieved the unique feat of winning the World Championship having competed for two different constructors in the same season (Maserati for rounds 1 & 3; Mercedes-Benz for rounds 4-7).
- Final Swiss Grand Prix: This was the final Formula One Grand Prix held on Swiss soil. Following the 1955 Le Mans disaster, the Swiss government banned all circuit motor racing in Switzerland.
- Stirling Moss Debut Mercedes Links: Stirling Moss finished 3rd in a private Maserati 250F, catching the attention of Mercedes racing director Alfred Neubauer.

## Records Set or Equalled
- Juan Manuel Fangio clinched his 2nd World Drivers' Championship.
- Only driver in F1 history to win the World Championship racing for two different constructors in the same season (Maserati and Mercedes).
- Final Swiss Grand Prix in World Championship history.
