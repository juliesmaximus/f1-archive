## Historical Context & Narrative Summary
The 1992 season finale marked the end of an era: Nigel Mansell's final Grand Prix before moving to American IndyCar racing, and the final race for Honda's factory Formula 1 engine program after a glorious decade of dominance.

Mansell claimed his 14th pole position of the season, setting an all-time Formula 1 single-season pole record that stood for 19 years until 2011. In the race, Mansell led Senna until lap 18, when Senna attempted an aggressive dive down the inside at the final hairpin, slamming into the rear of Mansell's Williams and eliminating both champions on the spot.

Gerhard Berger inherited the lead and fought off an intense late-race challenge from Michael Schumacher (who set the fastest lap on lap 68), crossing the line just 0.741 seconds ahead of the Benetton to gift Honda a victorious farewell.

## Lap Count & Aborted Start Reconciliation
- **81 vs 82 Laps:** An aborted start forced an extra formation lap. While automated classification timing systems on Formula1.com cataloged finishers with 82 laps to include the formation tour, the official FIA competitive race distance is 81 laps (306.180 km).
