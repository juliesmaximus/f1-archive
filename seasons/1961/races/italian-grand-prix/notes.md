- **Historic Name Notes**: 32nd Italian Grand Prix; 1961 World Championship title decider; marked by the catastrophic tragedy that claimed Wolfgang von Trips and 14 spectators.
- **Circuit Full Name**: Autodromo Nazionale di Monza (10 km Combined Banking)

## Verified Race Scenarios, Anomalies & Controversies
- The Catastrophe at Parabolica: Entering lap 2 on the approach to the Parabolica curve, championship leader Wolfgang von Trips (who needed only 3rd place to secure the World Championship) moved to defend his line against Jim Clark's Lotus. The wheels touched at over 140 mph. Von Trips' Ferrari veered left, bounced up the grass bank, struck the wire spectator fence, and somersaulted through the crowd. Wolfgang von Trips was thrown from the car and killed instantly, alongside 14 SPECTATORS. Over 60 spectators were injured in the deadlist crash in Formula One history.
- Race Not Stopped: In accordance with standard 1960s protocol to allow emergency services access and prevent mass panic, the race was not stopped.
- Phil Hill America's First World Champion: Phil Hill drove through the grief-stricken afternoon to win the race by 31.0 seconds over Dan Gurney, becoming the FIRST AMERICAN TO WIN THE FORMULA ONE WORLD DRIVERS' CHAMPIONSHIP.
- Ferrari Clinches First Constructors' Title: Scuderia Ferrari clinched their FIRST-EVER INTERNATIONAL CUP FOR F1 MANUFACTURERS (World Constructors' Championship).
- Permanent Abandonment of Monza Banking: Following the catastrophe, the high-speed concrete banking (*sopraelevata*) was PERMANENTLY RETIRED from Formula One racing.

## Records Set or Equalled
- Phil Hill crowned 1961 Formula One World Champion of Drivers (First American World Champion).
- Scuderia Ferrari clinched their first World Constructors' Championship (International Cup for F1 Manufacturers).
- Deadliest crash in Formula One World Championship history (15 fatalities including driver Wolfgang von Trips).
- Final Formula One race conducted on the 10.000 km Monza banked oval circuit.
