# Formula 1 World Championship Season 1967 - Final Standings

## World Drivers' Championship

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis to the top six finishers at each Grand Prix. Under the split-season dropped-score system, only the best **5** results from the first 6 races and the best **4** results from the final 5 races counted towards the final Drivers' Championship classification.

New Zealander Denny Hulme won his maiden World Drivers' Championship with 51 net points (51 gross), defeating his team boss Jack Brabham (46 net points / 48 gross, dropping 2 points from 5th place at the United States GP).

## International Cup for F1 Manufacturers (Constructors' Championship)

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis for the highest-finishing car of each manufacturer at each Grand Prix. Only the best **5** results from the first 6 races and the best **4** results from the final 5 races counted towards the championship.

Brabham-Repco won their second consecutive International Cup for F1 Manufacturers with 63 net points (67 gross).

## Regulatory Notes & Scoring System

- **Scoring System:** 9-6-4-3-2-1 for top 6 finishers.
- **Dropped Scores Rule:** Split into two mini-seasons: best 5 of the first 6 rounds, and best 4 of the final 5 rounds.
  - Jack Brabham scored 19 points in the first 6 races (all 4 finishes counted) and 29 points in the last 5 races (6, 9, 6, 2, 6; dropping 2 points from Watkins Glen 5th) to total 46 net points (48 gross).
  - Brabham-Repco scored 67 gross points, dropping 4 points to finish with 63 net points.
- **Engine Regulations:** 3.0-litre naturally aspirated or 1.5-litre supercharged/turbocharged. Minimum car weight 500 kg.
- **Historic Milestones:**
  - The revolutionary **Ford Cosworth DFV 3.0L V8** engine debuted at the Dutch Grand Prix (Zandvoort) in the Lotus 49, with Graham Hill taking pole and Jim Clark winning the race on debut.
  - Dan Gurney won the Belgian Grand Prix at Spa-Francorchamps in the Eagle-Weslake, becoming only the second driver (after Jack Brabham) to win a championship Grand Prix in a car of his own construction.
  - Denny Hulme won his sole World Drivers' Championship, becoming the first driver from New Zealand to achieve the feat.
  - Lorenzo Bandini suffered fatal burns following an accident at the chicane during the Monaco Grand Prix, prompting major subsequent revisions in circuit safety barriers and fire equipment.
