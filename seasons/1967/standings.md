# Formula 1 World Championship Season 1967 - Final Standings

## World Drivers' Championship

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis to the top six finishers at each Grand Prix. Under the split-season dropped-score system, only the best **5** results from the first 6 races and the best **4** results from the final 5 races counted towards the final Drivers' Championship classification.

New Zealander Denny Hulme won his maiden World Drivers' Championship with 51 net points (51 gross), defeating his team boss Jack Brabham (46 net points / 48 gross).

## International Cup for F1 Manufacturers (Constructors' Championship)

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis for the highest-finishing car of each manufacturer at each Grand Prix. Only the best **5** results from the first 6 races and best **4** results from the final 5 races counted towards the championship.

Brabham-Repco won their second consecutive International Cup for F1 Manufacturers with 63 net points (67 gross).

## Regulatory Notes & Scoring System

- **Scoring System:** 9-6-4-3-2-1 for top 6 finishers.
- **Dropped Scores Rule:** Split championship scoring system: best 5 results from rounds 1–6 and best 4 results from rounds 7–11 counted towards both championships.
- **Engine Revolution:** The debut of the Ford Cosworth DFV 3.0-litre V8 at Zandvoort, winning on debut with Jim Clark.
- **Historic Milestones:**
  - Denny Hulme became the first New Zealander to win the Formula One World Drivers' Championship.
  - Brabham-Repco won back-to-back Constructors' Championships (1966, 1967).
  - Dan Gurney and the Anglo American Racers Eagle T1G Weslake V12 won the Belgian Grand Prix at Spa, marking the only all-American F1 victory in Europe.
  - Lorenzo Bandini's fatal crash at Monaco led to the worldwide ban on straw bales and the mandatory introduction of fire-resistant Nomex racing suits.
  - John Surtees scored Honda's second Grand Prix victory in the RA300 at Monza by 0.2s after Jim Clark's historic recovery drive from a lap down.
