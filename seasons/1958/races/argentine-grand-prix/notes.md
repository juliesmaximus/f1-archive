# 1958 Argentine Grand Prix - Race Notes

## Official Race Identification
- **Official Name**: VI Gran Premio de la República Argentina
- **Round**: 1 of 11 in the 1958 FIA Formula One World Championship
- **Date**: Sunday, 19 January 1958
- **Location**: Autódromo 17 de Octubre, Buenos Aires, Argentina
- **Circuit Length**: 3.912 km (2.431 miles)
- **Distance**: 80 laps (312.960 km / 194.464 miles)

## Official Results & Key Metrics
- **Pole Position**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Maserati 250F) - 1:42.000
- **Fastest Lap**: Juan Manuel Fangio (Maserati 250F) - 1:41.800 (Lap 30) [1 pt]
- **Race Winner**: Stirling Moss (Cooper-Climax T43) - 2:19:33.700 (134.560 km/h)
- **Podium**:
  1. Stirling Moss (Cooper-Climax) - 80 laps
  2. Luigi Musso (Ferrari) - 80 laps (+2.700s)
  3. Mike Hawthorn (Ferrari) - 80 laps (+12.600s)

## Verified Race Scenarios & Primary-Source Facts
- **First Mid-Engine Victory in Championship History**: Stirling Moss, driving a tiny privateer 1.9-litre rear-engine Cooper-Climax entered by Rob Walker, stunned the factory Ferrari and Maserati teams. It was the first World Championship Grand Prix victory for a mid/rear-engine car, initiating the rear-engine design revolution in Formula One.
- **The Non-Stop Tire Gamble**: Vanwall boycotted the race due to fuel rule disputes. Rob Walker and Moss gambled on running the entire 80 laps without changing Continental tires. In the closing laps, Moss's tires were worn down to the canvas cords; he swerved onto oily and damp patches of track to cool the rubber, holding off Luigi Musso's charging Ferrari 246 Dino by just 2.7 seconds.
- **Abolition of Shared Drives**: Starting in 1958, shared car points were abolished; each driver had to complete the entire race distance in their own car to be classified and earn championship points.
- **Track & Weather Conditions**: Typical Buenos Aires midsummer heat and humidity; dry asphalt throughout.
