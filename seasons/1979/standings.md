# Formula 1 World Championship Season 1979 - Final Standings

## World Drivers' Championship

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis to the top six finishers at each Grand Prix. Under the split-season dropped-score system, only the best **4** results from the first 7 races and the best **4** results from the final 8 races counted towards the final Drivers' Championship classification.

Jody Scheckter won the 1979 FIA Formula One World Drivers' Championship driving the Ferrari 312T4, scoring 51 net points (60 gross). Teammate Gilles Villeneuve finished as runner-up with 47 net points (53 gross), sealing a dominant 1-2 championship finish for Scuderia Ferrari.

## International Cup for F1 Manufacturers (Constructors' Championship)

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis for all top six finishers of each constructor at each Grand Prix. Unlike previous seasons, **all** results counted towards the Constructors' Championship (no dropped scores), and multiple cars from the same constructor could score points.

Scuderia Ferrari captured their sixth International Cup for F1 Manufacturers with 113 points, winning 6 Grands Prix with the Ferrari 312T4. Williams Grand Prix Engineering finished runner-up with 75 points and 5 victories.

## Regulatory Notes & Scoring System

- **Scoring System:** 9-6-4-3-2-1 for top 6 finishers.
- **Drivers' Dropped Scores Rule:** Split scoring: best 4 results from rounds 1–7 and best 4 results from rounds 8–15 counted towards the Drivers' Championship.
- **Constructors' Scoring Rule:** For the first time, all scores counted towards the Constructors' Championship (no dropped rounds), and both cars of a team were eligible to score points.
- **Historic Milestones:**
  - Jody Scheckter won the World Drivers' Championship for Ferrari; Ferrari's last Drivers' Championship until Michael Schumacher in 2000.
  - Jean-Pierre Jabouille won the French GP at Dijon in the turbocharged Renault RS10, marking the historic first victory for a turbo-powered car in Formula One history.
  - The French GP featured the epic wheel-to-wheel duel between Gilles Villeneuve (Ferrari) and René Arnoux (Renault) for 2nd place.
  - Clay Regazzoni took Williams Grand Prix Engineering's first-ever Formula One victory at Silverstone.
  - Alan Jones won 4 of the final 6 races in the ground-effect Williams FW07.
  - Niki Lauda abruptly retired from Formula One during Friday practice at the Canadian GP, replaced on the spot by Ricardo Zunino.
