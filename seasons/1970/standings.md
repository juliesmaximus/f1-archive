# Formula 1 World Championship Season 1970 - Final Standings

## World Drivers' Championship

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis to the top six finishers at each Grand Prix. Under the split-season dropped-score system, only the best **6** results from the first 7 races and the best **5** results from the final 6 races counted towards the final Drivers' Championship classification.

Austrian Jochen Rindt won the World Drivers' Championship posthumously with 45 net points (45 gross), having won five Grands Prix before his fatal accident at Monza.

## International Cup for F1 Manufacturers (Constructors' Championship)

Points were awarded on a 9-6-4-3-2-1 basis for the highest-finishing car of each manufacturer at each Grand Prix. Only the best **6** results from the first 7 races and the best **5** results from the final 6 races counted towards the championship.

Team Lotus won the International Cup for F1 Manufacturers with 59 net points (63 gross).

## Regulatory Notes & Scoring System

- **Scoring System:** 9-6-4-3-2-1 for top 6 finishers.
- **Dropped Scores Rule:** Split into two mini-seasons: best 6 of first 7 rounds, best 5 of final 6 rounds.
  - Lotus-Ford scored 63 gross points, dropping 4 points to finish with 59 net points.
  - Ferrari scored 55 gross points, dropping 3 points to finish with 52 net points.
- **Engine Regulations:** 3.0-litre naturally aspirated or 1.5-litre supercharged/turbocharged. Minimum car weight 500 kg.
- **Historic Milestones:**
  - Jochen Rindt became the first and only posthumous World Drivers' Champion in Formula One history.
  - Jack Brabham scored his 14th and final career Grand Prix victory at Kyalami (South Africa).
  - Clay Regazzoni won his maiden Grand Prix at Monza in just his fifth Formula One start.
  - Emerson Fittipaldi took his maiden victory at the United States Grand Prix in only his fourth start, securing the Drivers' title for Rindt and the Constructors' title for Lotus.
  - Bruce McLaren was tragically killed on June 2 at Goodwood testing the McLaren M8D Can-Am car.
  - Piers Courage died tragically in a fiery crash during the Dutch Grand Prix at Zandvoort driving Frank Williams' De Tomaso.
