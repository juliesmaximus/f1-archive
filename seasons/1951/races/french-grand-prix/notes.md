# 1951 Round 4: 38e Grand Prix de l'ACF (Automobile Club de France) / Grand Prix d'Europe - Verified Race Record

## Circuit & Geographic Metadata
- **Official Race Title**: 38e Grand Prix de l'ACF (Automobile Club de France) / Grand Prix d'Europe
- **Historic Name Notes**: 38th Grand Prix organized by the Automobile Club de France, also designated with the honorary title 'Grand Prix d'Europe'; held at the fast Reims-Gueux circuit.
- **Circuit Full Name**: Reims-Gueux
- **Location**: Gueux, Marne, Champagne, France
- **Host Country**: France
- **Track Length**: 7.816 km (4.857 mi)
- **Track Altitude**: 88 m (289 ft) above sea level
- **Race Distance**: 77 laps / 601.832 km (373.961 mi)
- **Official Race Date**: 1951-07-01

## Verified Race Scenarios, Anomalies & Controversies
- **Shared Victory for Fagioli and Fangio**: Fangio started in car #24 but suffered ignition issues. At lap 20 pit stops, Alfa Romeo team manager ordered Luigi Fagioli to hand over his healthy #8 car to Fangio. Fangio mounted a charge through the field, reclaimed the lead, and took victory. Fagioli and Fangio split the 8 victory points (4 points each), with Fangio earning an additional 1 point for the fastest race lap (2:27.800).
- **Oldest Grand Prix Winner in History**: Luigi Fagioli (53 years, 22 days) became the oldest driver to win an official Formula One World Championship Grand Prix — an all-time record that remains unbroken.
- **Longest Grand Prix Distance**: With 77 laps around the 7.816 km circuit totaling 601.832 km (373.961 mi), this stands as the longest Grand Prix distance ever completed in Formula One World Championship history.
- **Shared Second Place**: Scuderia Ferrari likewise used shared drives: José Froilán González started #14 and handed it over to team leader Alberto Ascari on lap 35, finishing 2nd (+58.200s) and splitting 6 points (3 points each).
