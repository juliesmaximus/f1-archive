# Historic Engine Profile: BMW M12/13 Turbo

## Overview & Unrivaled Power Record
The **BMW M12/13** turbocharged 1.5-litre inline-four cylinder engine, engineered by BMW Motorsport maestro **Paul Rosche** (affectionately known as *"Nocken-Paul"* or "Camshaft Paul"), is the most powerful engine family in the entire history of Formula One motor racing.

Introduced to Grand Prix racing in 1982 with Brabham, the M12/13 achieved immortality in 1983 by powering Nelson Piquet to become the **first turbocharged World Drivers' Champion in Formula One history**. By 1986, in unrestricted qualifying trim running boost pressures exceeding 5.5 bar, the engine produced an estimated **1,400+ brake horsepower**—a figure so monstrous that BMW's dynamometers could not measure it directly.

---

## Technical Design & Origins

### 1. The Production Road Car Block
- Remarkably, the engine was built upon the standard cast-iron engine block of the **BMW M10**, a production road-car engine originally designed by Alex von Falkenhausen in 1961 for the BMW 1500 family sedan.
- Paul Rosche insisted on utilizing seasoned production blocks that had already covered over 100,000 kilometres on ordinary roads, reasoning that thermal cycling and age had fully relieved all casting stresses within the iron structure. Legends from the Brabham and BMW garages confirmed that blocks were often left out in the rain and frost at BMW's Preussenstrasse headquarters to cure.

### 2. Boost & Combustion Extremes
- **Configuration:** 1,499 cc (1.5 L), inline-4 cylinder, 16 valves, dual overhead camshafts (DOHC).
- **Induction:** Massive single KKK or Garrett turbocharger coupled to sophisticated air-to-air intercoolers.
- **Boost Pressures:**
  - 1982–1983: ~2.8 to 3.5 bar (~750–850 bhp race, 1,000 bhp qualifying).
  - 1986 (Peak Turbo Era): Up to **5.5 bar (80 psi)** of boost pressure in qualifying, generating over 1,400 bhp at 11,500 RPM.
- **Fuel:** Exotic toluene-based hydrocarbon fuel blends (developed by Wintershall/BASF) to prevent catastrophic pre-ignition and engine knock under astronomical cylinder pressures.

## Championship Success & Customer Cars
- **Constructors Powered:** Brabham (1982–1987), ATS (1983–1984), Arrows (1984–1986, continued as Megatron 1987–1988), Benetton (1986), Ligier (1987).
- **World Championships:** 1 World Drivers' Championship (Nelson Piquet, 1983, Brabham BT52).
- **Grand Prix Victories:** 9 (Piquet 7, Patrese 1, Gerhard Berger 1 [Benetton, Mexico 1986]).
- **The Fastest Lap Record:** At the 1986 Italian Grand Prix at Monza, Gerhard Berger's Benetton-BMW was clocked at a blistering **351.22 km/h (218.2 mph)** through the speed trap—the highest straight-line speed recorded in the 1.5-litre turbo era.

## Statistical Summary
- **Active Seasons:** 1982–1988 (as BMW and Megatron)
- **Grand Prix Starts:** Over 250 starts across customer entries
- **Wins:** 9 (BMW) + 1 (Megatron customer podiums)
- **Pole Positions:** 15
- **Fastest Laps:** 14
- **Highest Estimated Power:** 1,400–1,450 bhp (September 1986, Monza qualifying)
