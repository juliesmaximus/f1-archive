## Historical Significance

Peter Jeffrey Revson was one of the finest, most naturally gifted American road racers of the post-war era. An heir to the Revlon cosmetics fortune, Revson adamantly refused family financial backing, forging a fiercely independent career purely on driving merit. Handsome, articulate, and immensely brave, Revson conquered Formula One, Can-Am sports cars, and the Indianapolis 500.

---

## Career Highlights

### North American Glory & McLaren Arrival (1969–1972)
- Finished 2nd in the 1971 Indianapolis 500 after qualifying on pole position in a McLaren M16.
- Dominated the 1971 Can-Am Championship, winning five races for Team McLaren.
- Joined McLaren's Formula One team in 1972 alongside Denny Hulme, scoring four podiums and taking pole position at Mosport to finish 5th in the World Championship.

### Dual Grand Prix Winner (1973)
- In 1973, driving the Gordon Coppuck-designed McLaren M23, Revson reached the pinnacle of his Grand Prix career:
  - Scored his **maiden Grand Prix victory** at the 1973 British Grand Prix at Silverstone, fending off Ronnie Peterson by 2.8 seconds following the infamous Woodcote pileup.
  - Won the chaotic, rain-lashed 1973 Canadian Grand Prix at Mosport Park, holding off Emerson Fittipaldi in the race that featured the first-ever deployment of a Safety Car.
  - Finished 5th in the World Championship with 38 points.

### Tragic Accident at Kyalami (1974)
- Joined the Don Nichols-led Shadow team for 1974 to drive the new Tony Southgate-designed Shadow DN3.
- On March 22, 1974, during a test session prior to the South African Grand Prix at Kyalami, a front titanium suspension ball-joint failed at high speed through Barbeque Bend. The car speared straight into the Armco barrier and folded under the barrier, killing Revson instantly at age 35.
