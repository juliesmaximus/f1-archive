## Historical Significance

Carlos Alberto "Lole" Reutemann was Argentina's most successful Formula One driver after five-time World Champion Juan Manuel Fangio. Renowned for his brooding intensity, sublime precision, and devastating outright speed on his day, Reutemann was a 12-time Grand Prix winner and finished in the top three of the World Drivers' Championship four times (1975, 1978, 1980, 1981).

---

## Formula 1 Career Overview

### Brabham Breakthrough (1972–1976)
- Stunned Formula One on his World Championship debut by taking pole position at home in Buenos Aires in the Brabham BT34.
- Scored his maiden victory at Kyalami in 1974 in Gordon Murray's Brabham BT44, followed by wins at the Österreichring and Watkins Glen.
- Won the 1975 German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring Nordschleife, finishing 3rd overall in the World Championship.

### Scuderia Ferrari (1976–1978)
- Joined Ferrari following Niki Lauda's 1976 crash, winning in Brazil in 1977.
- In 1978, partnered Gilles Villeneuve in the Ferrari 312T3 on Michelin radial tyres, winning four Grands Prix (Brazil, Long Beach, Brands Hatch, Watkins Glen) to finish 3rd in the championship.

### Williams & The 1981 Title Heartbreak (1980–1982)
- Joined Williams alongside Alan Jones in 1980, winning the Monaco Grand Prix and taking eight podiums to help Williams win their first Constructors' Championship.
- In 1981, Reutemann defied team orders at Rio to win the Brazilian Grand Prix and won again at Zolder. Leading the championship into the Caesars Palace finale in Las Vegas, Reutemann suffered severe gearbox handling problems, finishing 8th and cruelly losing the World Championship by a single point to Nelson Piquet (50 pts vs. 49 pts).
- Retired from Formula One early in 1982 following the South Atlantic (Falklands) conflict.

### Post-Racing Career
- Transitioned into Argentine politics, serving two highly respected terms as Governor of Santa Fe Province and as a federal National Senator from 2003 until his death in 2021.
