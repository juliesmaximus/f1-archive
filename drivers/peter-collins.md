## Career Summary

Peter John Collins (6 November 1931 – 3 August 1958) was an English racing driver celebrated for his speed, competitive victories, and an extraordinary act of sportsmanship at the 1956 Italian Grand Prix that defined his legacy in Formula 1 history.

After making his World Championship debut with HWM in 1952 and driving for Vanwall and Maserati, Collins signed for Scuderia Ferrari in 1956, driving the Vittorio Jano-designed Lancia-Ferrari D50. He enjoyed immediate success, winning back-to-back victories at the 1956 Belgian Grand Prix at Spa-Francorchamps and the 1956 French Grand Prix at Reims.

Entering the final round at Monza in 1956, Collins was mathematically capable of winning the World Championship if he won the race with fastest lap and Juan Manuel Fangio failed to score. When Fangio retired with broken steering arm on lap 30, Ferrari teammate Luigi Musso refused to hand over his car to Fangio. When Collins pitted for fresh tyres on lap 35, he voluntarily offered his car to Fangio. Fangio took over the D50, finished 2nd behind Stirling Moss, and clinched his 4th World Drivers' Championship. Collins and Fangio shared the 6 points for 2nd place (3 points each), dropping Collins to 3rd in the championship. Collins famously remarked that Fangio deserved the championship, and that at age 24 he had plenty of time to win titles of his own.

Tragically, Collins was killed just two years later at the 1958 German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring Nordschleife on 3 August 1958, weeks after winning his home 1958 British Grand Prix at Silverstone.
