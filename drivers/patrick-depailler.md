# Driver Profile: Patrick Depailler

## Overview & Career Summary
Patrick André Eugène Joseph Depailler (August 9, 1944 – August 1, 1980) was a gifted and charismatic French racing driver who played an instrumental role in the mid-to-late 1970s Formula One World Championship, primarily as the stalwart team leader of Elf Team Tyrrell.

Renowned for his raw speed, feedback, and bravery—most notably in mastering and developing the radical six-wheeled Tyrrell P34—Depailler overcame numerous near-misses and podium finishes to claim an emotional maiden victory at the 1978 Monaco Grand Prix and a second win at the 1979 Spanish Grand Prix.

---

## Formula One Career

### The Tyrrell Era & The Six-Wheeled P34 (1974–1978)
- Joined Ken Tyrrell's works team full-time in 1974 alongside Jody Scheckter following the retirement of Jackie Stewart and the death of François Cevert. Scored his first pole position at Anderstorp in 1974.
- **The Six-Wheeler (1976–1977):** Depailler became the greatest champion of Derek Gardner's revolutionary six-wheeled Tyrrell P34, scoring 5 second-place finishes in 1976 and finishing 4th in the World Drivers' Championship.
- **Maiden Monaco Victory (1978):** In the Tyrrell 008, Depailler ended years of near-misses (having scored 15 podiums without a win) by delivering a commanding drive through the streets of Monte Carlo to take his **maiden Formula One World Championship victory** on May 7, 1978.

### Ligier Dominance & Hang-Gliding Accident (1979)
- Joined Guy Ligier's French all-star team alongside Jacques Laffite in 1979 to drive the ground-effect Ligier JS11.
- Won the 1979 Spanish Grand Prix at Jarama in dominant fashion and tied for the World Championship lead after five rounds.
- His championship bid was cut short in June 1979 when he suffered severe compound leg fractures in a hang-gliding accident near his hometown of Clermont-Ferrand.

### Alfa Romeo Return & Tragedy at Hockenheim (1980)
- Returned to Formula One in 1980 with Autodelta Alfa Romeo to lead development of the V12-powered Alfa Romeo 179, defying intense physical pain from his healing legs.
- On August 1, 1980, during private testing at the high-speed Hockenheimring ten days prior to the German Grand Prix, Depailler suffered a fatal crash at the Ostkurve following suspension or skirt failure.
