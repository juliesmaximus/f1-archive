# Driver Profile: Gilles Villeneuve

## Overview & Immortal Legacy
Joseph Gilles Henri Villeneuve (January 18, 1950 – May 8, 1982) was a legendary Canadian racing driver whose extraordinary car control, fearless racecraft, and romantic passion earned him mythical status in the history of Formula One. Beloved by Enzo Ferrari as an adoptive son and revered by fans worldwide, Villeneuve embodied pure, unfiltered racing spirit.

Over a six-season Grand Prix career spent almost entirely with Scuderia Ferrari, Villeneuve took 6 victories and 13 podiums. His iconic wheel-to-wheel duel with René Arnoux at Dijon in 1979 and his three-wheeled drive at Zandvoort in 1979 remain among the most celebrated moments in motorsport history. The Canadian Grand Prix venue in Montreal was permanently renamed **Circuit Gilles Villeneuve** in his memory.

## Formula One Career

### Breakthrough & Enzo Ferrari's Call (1977)
- After dominating North American Formula Atlantic and snowmobile racing, Villeneuve made an astonishing Formula One debut at the 1977 British Grand Prix at Silverstone driving an older McLaren M23, qualifying 9th and running 7th while displaying breathtaking drifts.
- Enzo Ferrari, captivated by Villeneuve's raw speed and reminiscent of Tazio Nuvolari, signed him to replace Niki Lauda for the final two races of 1977 in Canada and Japan.

### Maiden Victory on Home Soil (1978)
- Partnering Carlos Reutemann in the Ferrari 312T3, Villeneuve learned European circuits and scored his first podium (3rd) at a rain-soaked Österreichring.
- **Historic Montreal Victory:** At the inaugural Grand Prix on the new Île Notre-Dame circuit in Montreal, Villeneuve claimed his **maiden Formula One victory** in freezing late autumn temperatures before 72,000 ecstatic Canadian fans, presented the winner's trophy by Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau.

### The 1979 Title Fight & The Dijon Epic (1979)
- Driving the flat-12 ground-effect Ferrari 312T4, Villeneuve formed a close bond with teammate Jody Scheckter.
- Villeneuve won 3 Grands Prix (Kyalami, Long Beach, Watkins Glen) and finished **runner-up in the 1979 World Drivers' Championship** (47 net points to Scheckter's 51), loyally honoring Ferrari team orders at Monza to ensure Scheckter and Ferrari won the World Championships.
- **The Battle of Dijon (1979 French GP):** Villeneuve and Renault's René Arnoux engaged in what is universally regarded as the most electrifying wheel-to-wheel battle in F1 history, banging wheels and swapping second place multiple times over the final three laps, with Villeneuve prevailing by 0.24 seconds.

### The Turbo Struggles & Imola Heartbreak (1980–1982)
- Dragged the uncompetitive Ferrari 312T5 in 1980 and performed miracles in the powerful but ill-handling turbocharged Ferrari 126CK in 1981, winning against all odds on the tight street circuits of Monaco and Jarama (holding off a four-car train for 67 laps).
- **1982 San Marino GP at Imola:** Led teammate Didier Pironi under team orders ("SLOW"), but Pironi passed him on the final lap to take victory. Bitterly betrayed, Villeneuve vowed never to speak to Pironi again.

### Tragedy at Zolder (May 8, 1982)
- Two weeks later during final qualifying for the 1982 Belgian Grand Prix at Zolder, Villeneuve crashed heavily while attempting to beat Pironi's qualifying time, sustaining fatal head and neck injuries.

## Statistical Summary
- **Grand Prix Starts:** 67
- **Wins:** 6 (Canada 1978, South Africa 1979, Long Beach 1979, United States 1979, Monaco 1981, Spain 1981)
- **Podiums:** 13
- **Pole Positions:** 2
- **Fastest Laps:** 8
- **Career Points:** 101 (107 gross)
- **Championship Best:** 2nd in World Drivers' Championship (1979)
- **First Race:** 1977 British Grand Prix (Silverstone)
- **Final Race:** 1982 San Marino Grand Prix (Imola)
