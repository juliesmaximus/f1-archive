# Driver Profile: Alain Prost

## Overview & Historic Legacy
Alain Marie Pascal Prost OBE (born February 24, 1955) is a French racing legend and **four-time FIA Formula One World Drivers' Champion** (1985, 1986, 1989, 1993). Revered worldwide as "The Professor" (*Le Professeur*), Prost revolutionized Grand Prix racing by elevating intellectual racecraft, tactical restraint, and engineering precision to an art form.

Prost held the all-time Formula One records for most victories (51) and most podium finishes (106) for over a decade following his retirement. His epochal rivalry with McLaren teammate Ayrton Senna is universally regarded as the fiercest, most intense psychological and sporting duel in motorsport history.

## Formula One Career

### Breakthrough & Renault Turbo Supremacy (1980–1983)
- Debuted with McLaren in 1980, scoring a championship point on debut (6th in Argentina).
- Joined Equipe Renault in 1981 to pilot the turbocharged RE30.
- **Maiden Grand Prix Victory (1981 French GP, Dijon-Prenois):** Won in front of his home crowd, taking 9 victories for Renault between 1981 and 1983. Finished runner-up in the 1983 World Championship by only two points to Nelson Piquet before departing after bitter disputes with Renault management.

### The McLaren Golden Era & World Titles (1984–1989)
- Rejoined Ron Dennis's McLaren alongside double World Champion Niki Lauda in 1984. Prost won 7 races but lost the World Championship to Lauda by just **0.5 points** (the closest title finish in F1 history).
- **1985 World Championship:** Dominated in the John Barnard-designed, TAG-Porsche V6 turbo-powered McLaren MP4/2B, scoring 73 points to become **France's first-ever Formula One World Champion**.
- **1986 World Championship:** Defied the faster Williams-Honda duo of Mansell and Piquet, driving a tactical masterpiece to win in Adelaide and become the first back-to-back champion since Jack Brabham (1960).
- **1987 Victory Record:** Won the Portuguese GP at Estoril, scoring his 28th career victory to surpass Sir Jackie Stewart's long-standing all-time win record.

### The Senna-Prost War (1988–1989)
- McLaren paired Prost with Brazilian sensation Ayrton Senna in 1988 with the all-conquering Honda V6 turbo MP4/4 (winning 15 of 16 races). Prost scored more gross points (105 to 94), but Senna took the title on drop scores (90 to 87).
- **1989 World Championship & Suzuka Collision:** Tensions exploded into outright psychological war. At the penultimate round at Suzuka, Senna lunged inside at the chicane on lap 47; Prost turned in, resulting in a collision. Prost retired on the spot, while Senna restarted through the escape road and was disqualified, securing Prost his **third World Drivers' Championship**.

### Scuderia Ferrari (1990–1991) & Williams Finale (1993)
- Joined Ferrari in 1990, winning 5 races in the 641 and taking the title fight to Suzuka, where Senna deliberately drove into Prost at 160 mph at Turn 1 to secure the championship.
- Took a sabbatical in 1992 before returning in 1993 with Williams-Renault, dominating in the active-suspension FW15C with 7 wins to capture his **fourth World Drivers' Championship** before retiring permanently at the top of the sport.

## Statistical Summary
- **World Championships:** 4 (1985, 1986, 1989, 1993)
- **Grand Prix Starts:** 199
- **Wins:** 51
- **Podiums:** 106
- **Pole Positions:** 33
- **Fastest Laps:** 41
- **Career Points:** 768.5 (798.5 gross)
- **First Race:** 1980 Argentine Grand Prix (Buenos Aires)
- **First Win:** 1981 French Grand Prix (Dijon-Prenois)
- **Final Win:** 1993 German Grand Prix (Hockenheim)
- **Final Race:** 1993 Australian Grand Prix (Adelaide)
