# Ayrton Senna — Career Statistics & Profile

Ayrton Senna da Silva (21 March 1960 – 1 May 1994) was a Brazilian racing driver who won three Formula One World Drivers' Championships for McLaren (1988, 1990, 1991). Widely considered one of the greatest racing drivers in motorsport history, Senna was legendary for his raw qualifying speed, extraordinary wet-weather mastery, and fierce competitive rivalry with Alain Prost.

---

## Career Summary

- **World Drivers' Championships**: **3** (1988, 1990, 1991)
- **First Grand Prix**: 1984 Brazilian Grand Prix (Jacarepaguá, Toleman-Hart TG183B)
- **First Grand Prix Win**: 1985 Portuguese Grand Prix (Estoril, Lotus-Renault 97T — legendary wet-weather masterclass)
- **Final Grand Prix Win**: 1993 Australian Grand Prix (Adelaide, McLaren-Ford MP4/8)
- **Final Grand Prix**: 1994 San Marino Grand Prix (Imola, Williams-Renault FW16, 1 May 1994)
- **Total Grand Prix Starts**: 161
- **Total Grand Prix Wins**: **41** (35 for McLaren, 6 for Lotus)
- **Total Pole Positions**: **65**
- **Total Podiums**: **80**
- **Total Career Points**: 610 (gross) / 614 (net under dropped score rules)

## All-Time Historical Milestones
1. **The Master of Monaco**: Won 6 Monaco Grands Prix, including 5 consecutive wins between 1989 and 1993.
2. **Consecutive Pole Positions**: Set the all-time record of **8 consecutive pole positions** (1988 Spanish GP to 1989 United States GP), an outright record later matched by Max Verstappen in 2023–2024.
3. **Monaco 1988 Qualifying Lap**: Qualified 1.427 seconds faster than teammate Alain Prost in identical equipment, describing it as driving on a different plane of consciousness.
4. **Donington Park 1993 Opening Lap**: Widely voted the "Lap of the Century", overtaking Schumacher, Wendlinger, Hill, and Prost on Lap 1 in torrential rain.
