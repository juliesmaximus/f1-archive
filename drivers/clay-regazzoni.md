# Driver Profile: Clay Regazzoni

## Overview & Historic Legacy
Gianclaudio Giuseppe "Clay" Regazzoni (September 5, 1939 – December 15, 2006) was a charismatic, fearless Swiss racing driver and a beloved icon of 1970s Formula One. Renowned for his swashbuckling racecraft, razor-sharp reflexes, and immense popularity among the Tifosi, Regazzoni was an instrumental figure for Scuderia Ferrari, BRM, Ensign, Shadow, and Williams.

Regazzoni won 5 World Championship Grands Prix, secured 28 podiums, and finished as **runner-up in the 1974 World Drivers' Championship** by just 3 points. In 1979, he etched his name permanently into motorsport history by delivering **Williams Grand Prix Engineering's very first Formula One victory** at the British Grand Prix at Silverstone.

---

## Formula One Career

### Sensational Ferrari Debut & Monza Glory (1970)
- Promoted by Enzo Ferrari midway through the 1970 season, Regazzoni finished 4th on debut at Zandvoort.
- In only his fifth World Championship start, Regazzoni drove a spellbinding race to win the **1970 Italian Grand Prix at Monza**, sending over 100,000 Tifosi into absolute hysteria. He finished 3rd in the World Championship in his rookie year.

### The 1974 Title Fight & The Lauda Partnership (1974–1976)
- Returned to Ferrari in 1974 alongside new teammate Niki Lauda to lead the team's resurrection under Luca di Montezemolo and Mauro Forghieri.
- Won the 1974 German Grand Prix at the 14-mile Nürburgring Nordschleife. Regazzoni entered the final race at Watkins Glen tied on points with Emerson Fittipaldi, ultimately finishing **runner-up in the 1974 World Drivers' Championship**.
- Won the 1975 Italian Grand Prix at Monza on the day Ferrari clinched their first Constructors' title in 11 years, and won the 1976 United States GP West at Long Beach from pole.

### Historic Maiden Victory for Williams (1979)
- Signed by Frank Williams and Patrick Head to partner Alan Jones in 1979 with the ground-effect Williams FW07.
- **The Silverstone Miracle:** At the 1979 British Grand Prix at Silverstone, Regazzoni took over the lead following Jones's retirement to score **Williams Grand Prix Engineering's FIRST EVER FORMULA ONE VICTORY**, finishing 24.28 seconds ahead of René Arnoux. He scored four additional podiums to finish 5th in the championship.

### The Long Beach Crash & Paraplegic Advocacy (1980–2006)
- Joined Ensign in 1980. At the 1980 Long Beach Grand Prix, Regazzoni's brake pedal snapped at 170 mph at the end of Shoreline Drive, crashing into Ricardo Zunino's retired Brabham and the concrete barriers. Regazzoni suffered severe spinal injuries that left him paralyzed from the waist down.
- Refusing to abandon motorsport, Regazzoni competed with hand controls in the Paris-Dakar Rally, the Sebring 12 Hours, and historic racing, becoming a globally admired pioneer for disabled accessibility and rehabilitation.

## Statistical Summary
- **Grand Prix Starts:** 132
- **Wins:** 5 (Italy 1970, Germany 1974, Italy 1975, Long Beach 1976, Great Britain 1979)
- **Podiums:** 28
- **Pole Positions:** 5
- **Fastest Laps:** 15
- **Career Points:** 209 (212 gross)
- **Championship Best:** 2nd in World Drivers' Championship (1974)
- **First Race:** 1970 Dutch Grand Prix (Zandvoort)
- **First Win:** 1970 Italian Grand Prix (Monza)
- **Final Win:** 1979 British Grand Prix (Silverstone)
- **Final Race:** 1980 United States Grand Prix West (Long Beach)
