## Career Summary & American Pioneer

Philip Toll Hill Jr. (20 April 1927 – 28 August 2008) was an American racing driver celebrated for his supreme mechanical sympathy, calm precision in the cockpit, and courage across sports cars and Grand Prix racing. He remains one of the towering figures of American motorsport history.

### The Scuderia Ferrari Era (1958–1962)

Signed by Enzo Ferrari in 1958 initially for sports car endurance racing, Hill quickly earned a seat in the Formula One team alongside Mike Hawthorn and Peter Collins:
- **1958 24 Hours of Le Mans**: Hill scored his first of three overall Le Mans victories, partnering Belgian ace Olivier Gendebien in the rain-drenched Ferrari 250 TR.
- **1960 Italian Grand Prix**: Hill scored his maiden World Championship victory, becoming the **FIRST AMERICAN-BORN DRIVER TO WIN A FORMULA ONE GRAND PRIX**. By doing so in the Ferrari Dino 246, Hill also achieved the **LAST-EVER FORMULA ONE GRAND PRIX VICTORY FOR A FRONT-ENGINE CAR**.
- **1961 World Championship**: Driving Carlo Chiti's iconic 'Sharknose' Ferrari 156 V6, Hill fought a season-long duel against German teammate Wolfgang von Trips. After winning at Spa, Hill entered the Italian Grand Prix at Monza trailing von Trips. Following von Trips' tragic fatal crash on lap 2, Hill drove through grief to win the race and become **AMERICA'S FIRST FORMULA ONE WORLD DRIVERS' CHAMPION**.
- **The Historic Double**: By winning both the Formula One World Championship and the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1961, Phil Hill achieved a feat unmatched in motorsport history: **THE ONLY DRIVER TO WIN THE F1 WORLD CHAMPIONSHIP AND LE MANS IN THE SAME CALENDAR YEAR**.

Hill retired from Grand Prix racing in 1966, concluding his professional career with victories in the Chaparral 2D and 2F sports cars before becoming an internationally acclaimed classic car restorer, judge at the Pebble Beach Concours d'Elegance, and automotive journalist.
