# Driver Profile: Elio de Angelis

## Overview & Tragic Legacy
Elio de Angelis (March 26, 1958 – May 15, 1986) was a cultured, blisteringly fast, and deeply respected Italian racing driver often referred to as Formula One's "last gentleman racer". A concert-standard classical pianist born into a wealthy Roman family, de Angelis combined aristocratic grace with fierce competitiveness and clinical racecraft.

De Angelis achieved 2 Grand Prix victories and 9 podiums, notably scoring the **final victory for Team Lotus founder Colin Chapman** at the 1982 Austrian Grand Prix by a breathtaking 0.050 seconds. His tragic death in a private testing accident at Circuit Paul Ricard in May 1986 galvanized the Grand Prix Drivers' Association and forced Formula One to mandate full-scale track safety, fire marshals, and medical helicopters for private testing sessions.

## Formula One Career

### Shadow & The Colin Chapman Lotus Era (1979–1985)
- Debuted with Shadow in 1979, scoring 4th place at Watkins Glen in atrocious wet conditions.
- Signed by Colin Chapman for Team Lotus in 1980 to partner Mario Andretti. Scored 2nd place in Brazil in only his second race for Lotus, finishing 7th in the championship.
- **The Österreichring Epic (1982 Austrian GP):** In the Lotus 91 Ford DFV, de Angelis inherited the lead on lap 48 and resisted a ferocious charge from Keke Rosberg's Williams over the final two laps. De Angelis crossed the finish line to take his **maiden Formula One victory by 0.050 seconds**—one of the closest finishes in history. Colin Chapman ran onto the track and tossed his cap into the air for the final time.
- **Renault Turbo Era & Imola Victory (1983–1985):** Developed the Lotus-Renault turbo series (94T, 95T, 97T). Finished 3rd in the 1984 World Drivers' Championship behind only the dominant McLaren-TAG duo of Lauda and Prost.
- **1985 San Marino GP (Imola):** Scored his second career victory after Alain Prost was disqualified for an underweight car. Out-qualified and raced closely alongside rookie Lotus teammate Ayrton Senna throughout 1985 before departing due to Senna's favored team status.

### Brabham & The Paul Ricard Tragedy (1986)
- Joined Bernie Ecclestone's Parmalat Brabham team in 1986 to drive Gordon Murray's radical low-line "skateboard" Brabham BT55 powered by the tilted BMW turbo 4-cylinder engine.
- On May 14, 1986, during a private testing session at Circuit Paul Ricard in France, the rear wing of de Angelis's BT55 detached at 180 mph entering the fast Virage de la Verrerie. The car cartwheeled over the barriers, landed upside-down, and caught fire.
- Due to the lack of track marshals, fire trucks, or emergency helicopters at private test sessions, drivers Alan Jones, Alain Prost, and Nigel Mansell had to fight the flames with inadequate hand extinguishers. De Angelis suffered severe smoke inhalation and oxygen deprivation, passing away the following day in hospital in Marseille.

## Statistical Summary
- **Grand Prix Starts:** 108
- **Wins:** 2 (Austria 1982, San Marino 1985)
- **Podiums:** 9
- **Pole Positions:** 3
- **Career Points:** 122
- **Championship Best:** 3rd in World Drivers' Championship (1984)
- **First Race:** 1979 Argentine Grand Prix (Buenos Aires)
- **First Win:** 1982 Austrian Grand Prix (Österreichring)
- **Final Win:** 1985 San Marino Grand Prix (Imola)
- **Final Race:** 1986 Monaco Grand Prix (Monte Carlo)
