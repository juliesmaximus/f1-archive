# Driver Profile: Alan Jones

## Overview & Career Summary
Alan Stanley Jones MBE (born 2 November 1946) is an Australian racing driver who won the **1980 FIA Formula One World Drivers' Championship**, becoming the second Australian to claim the world title after Sir Jack Brabham (1959, 1960, 1966).

Renowned for his uncompromisingly aggressive, wheel-to-wheel racecraft and physical toughness, Jones scored his sensational maiden victory at the 1977 Austrian Grand Prix for the Shadow team before spearheading the rise of Sir Frank Williams's team into an F1 powerhouse, delivering Williams Grand Prix Engineering its first World Championship double in 1980.

---

## Formula One Career

### Breakthrough with Shadow (1977)
- Following the tragic death of Shadow team leader Tom Pryce at Kyalami in March 1977, team manager Jackie Oliver signed Jones as replacement driver alongside Riccardo Patrese.
- **Maiden Grand Prix Win (1977 Austrian GP, Österreichring):** Starting 14th on the grid in tricky damp-drying conditions at the ultra-fast Österreichring, Jones mounted a relentless charge through the pack in the Shadow DN8 Ford Cosworth DFV, passing James Hunt and inheriting the lead when Gunnar Nilsson's engine failed to win by 20.13 seconds over Niki Lauda. It was Shadow's only World Championship victory.
- Jones finished 7th in the 1977 World Championship with 22 points.

### The Williams Dynasty & World Championship (1978–1981)
- **1978 Foundation:** Frank Williams and technical director Patrick Head signed Jones as the sole driver for the new Saudia-sponsored Williams FW06. Jones scored Williams's first podium with 2nd at Watkins Glen.
- **1979 Ascendancy:** In Patrick Head's revolutionary ground-effect Williams FW07, Jones dominated the second half of the season, winning 4 of the last 6 races (Germany, Austria, Netherlands, Canada) to finish 3rd in the championship.
- **1980 World Championship:** Jones delivered a masterclass campaign, winning 5 Grands Prix (Argentina, France, Great Britain, Canada, United States) in the Williams FW07B to score 67 points, securing both the **World Drivers' Championship** and Williams's **first World Constructors' Championship**.
- **1981 Contention:** Jones won 2 races (Long Beach, Caesars Palace) and finished 3rd in the standings with 46 points before announcing his first retirement.

### Later Career & Returns (1983, 1985–1986)
- Made a brief return with Arrows in 1983 and drove for the Haas Beatrice Lola team with turbocharged Hart and Ford V6 engines in 1985–1986 before retiring permanently from Formula One.
