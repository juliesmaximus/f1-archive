## Career Summary & The Triple Crown

Norman Graham Hill (15 February 1929 – 29 November 1975) was an English racing driver, team owner, and one of the most charismatic and revered figures in motor racing history. Celebrated for his razor-sharp wit, dapper moustache, and relentless determination, he is the **ONLY DRIVER IN MOTORSPORT HISTORY TO ACHIEVE THE 'TRIPLE CROWN OF MOTORSPORT'**:
1. **Formula One World Championship** (1962, 1968) and **Monaco Grand Prix** (1963, 1964, 1965, 1968, 1969)
2. **Indianapolis 500** (1966)
3. **24 Hours of Le Mans** (1972)

### BRM Maiden World Championship (1962)

After debuting with Team Lotus in 1958, Hill joined British Racing Motors (BRM) in 1960. In 1962, driving Tony Rudd's exquisite BRM P57 V8, Hill scored four victories (Netherlands, Germany, Italy, South Africa) to defeat Jim Clark and deliver **BRM's FIRST WORLD DRIVERS' AND CONSTRUCTORS' CHAMPIONSHIPS**.

### 'Mr. Monaco' & The 1968 Lotus Triumph

Hill earned the legendary nickname **'Mr. Monaco'** by winning the Monaco Grand Prix five times (1963, 1964, 1965, 1968, 1969), an all-time record that stood for 24 years until Ayrton Senna won his sixth in 1993.

In 1967, Hill rejoined Colin Chapman at Team Lotus alongside Jim Clark:
- **1968 Title Leadership**: Following the tragic death of team leader Jim Clark in an F2 race at Hockenheim in April 1968, Hill rallied the grief-stricken Lotus team around him. Driving the revolutionary high-winged Lotus 49B-Ford Cosworth DFV, Hill scored emotional victories at Spain, Monaco, and Mexico to claim his **Second World Drivers' Championship**.

### The Embassy Hill Team & Tragedy

In 1973, Hill established his own Formula One team, Embassy Racing with Graham Hill. After failing to qualify at Monaco in 1975, Hill retired from driving to concentrate on team management, nurturing young British talent Tony Brise.

Tragically, on 29 November 1975, while returning from a test session at Circuit Paul Ricard in France, the light aircraft piloted by Hill crashed in thick fog on the Arkley golf course near London. Graham Hill, driver Tony Brise, and four team personnel were killed instantly.

Graham's son Damon Hill won the Formula One World Championship in 1996, making Graham and Damon the **first father-and-son World Champions in Formula One history**.
