## Historical Significance

José Carlos Pace, affectionately known as *"Moco"*, was one of Brazil's finest racing drivers. A hero of Paulistano motorsport alongside Emerson Fittipaldi, Pace possessed searing natural speed, beautiful car control, and an infectious warmth. In his honour, Brazil's premier Grand Prix circuit, **Interlagos in São Paulo, was officially renamed the Autódromo José Carlos Pace**.

---

## Formula 1 Career Overview

### Early Years (1972–1974)
- Debuted with Frank Williams Racing in 1972, scoring his first points at Jarama (6th).
- Moved to John Surtees' team in 1973, scoring his maiden podium at the Österreichring (3rd) and setting the fastest lap at the Nürburgring.
- Joined Bernie Ecclestone's Brabham team mid-way through 1974 to drive Gordon Murray's Brabham BT44 alongside Carlos Reutemann, scoring 2nd at Watkins Glen in a Brabham 1-2 sweep.

### Peak Glory with Brabham (1975–1977)
- Reached the summit of his career on January 26, 1975, at Interlagos: driving the Brabham BT44B, Pace swept to his **maiden Formula One victory** before 120,000 delirious fans, finishing 5.6s ahead of Emerson Fittipaldi for a historic Brazilian 1-2 finish.
- Took pole position at Kyalami and scored podiums at Monaco and Silverstone, finishing 6th in the 1975 World Championship with 24 points.
- Helped Brabham develop the complex Alfa Romeo Flat-12 engine package throughout 1976.
- Started 1976 in sublime form, finishing 2nd in the season opener in Argentina.

### Tragic Passing & Eternal Legacy
- On March 18, 1977, just days after the South African Grand Prix, Pace was killed in a light aircraft crash alongside pilot Marivaldo Fernandes during a severe rainstorm near Mairiporã, just outside São Paulo. He was 32 years old.
- The motorsport world, and Brazil in particular, was devastated. In tribute to his beloved memory, the Interlagos circuit was officially named the **Autódromo José Carlos Pace**, immortalizing his name at the home of the Brazilian Grand Prix.
