# Driver Profile: Michele Alboreto

## Overview & Historic Legacy
Michele Alboreto (December 23, 1956 – April 25, 2001) was a beloved Italian racing driver who became one of Italy's greatest modern Grand Prix icons. Renowned for his calm technical authority, exquisite racecraft, and gentlemanly dignity, Alboreto was the first Italian driver signed by Enzo Ferrari to a full-time works seat in over a decade (1984–1988).

Over 194 Grand Prix starts, Alboreto scored 5 victories and 23 podiums. In 1985, he led the World Championship in the Ferrari 156/85, ultimately finishing as **runner-up in the FIA Formula One World Drivers' Championship** behind Alain Prost. Later in his career, he triumphed at the 24 Hours of Le Mans (1997) before losing his life in a testing crash with Audi in 2001.

---

## Formula One Career

### Tyrrell Underdog Victories (1981–1983)
- Signed by Ken Tyrrell in 1981, Alboreto scored Tyrrell's first win in four years at the **1982 Caesars Palace Grand Prix** in Las Vegas in the naturally aspirated Tyrrell 011.
- **The Cosworth DFV's Final Win (1983 Detroit GP):** Around the bumpy streets of Detroit, Alboreto drove a flawless race to score his second victory, taking what proved to be the **155th and final Formula One victory for the legendary Ford Cosworth DFV engine** in modern F1 history.

### Scuderia Ferrari & The 1985 Championship Fight (1984–1988)
- Enzo Ferrari signed Alboreto for 1984 to partner René Arnoux. Alboreto won the 1984 Belgian Grand Prix at Zolder from pole position, becoming the first Italian to win for Ferrari since Ludovico Scarfiotti in 1966.
- **1985 Title Challenge:** In Harvey Postlethwaite's Ferrari 156/85 turbo, Alboreto drove masterfully, winning the Canadian Grand Prix in Montreal and the German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring. After 11 rounds, Alboreto led the World Championship over Alain Prost. However, late-season turbo and clutch reliability failures cost him the title, finishing **2nd in the World Drivers' Championship** with 53 points.
- Led an emotional Ferrari 1-2 at Monza in 1988 behind teammate Gerhard Berger, just three weeks after the death of founder Enzo Ferrari.

### Later Teams & Le Mans Triumph (1989–2001)
- Drove for Tyrrell, Larrousse, Footwork/Arrows, and Minardi through 1994, achieving 194 career starts.
- Transitioned to sports car racing, winning the **1997 24 Hours of Le Mans** with Joest Racing in the TWR Porsche WSC-95 alongside Stefan Johansson and Tom Kristensen.
- On April 25, 2001, during high-speed testing of the Audi R8 Le Mans prototype at the EuroSpeedway Lausitz in Germany, Alboreto suffered a fatal crash at 200 mph following a catastrophic tire blowout.
