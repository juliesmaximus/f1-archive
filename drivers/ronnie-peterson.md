## Historical Significance

Bengt Ronnie Peterson, universally revered by fans and peers as *"SuperSwede"*, was widely acknowledged as the fastest, most naturally gifted car controller in Formula One history. Renowned for breathtaking high-speed opposite-lock slides and sublime natural balance, Peterson was a two-time World Championship runner-up and a ten-time Grand Prix winner.

---

## Formula 1 Career Overview

### March Engineering & Breakthrough Runner-Up (1970–1972)
- Made his Grand Prix debut at Monaco in 1970 driving a customer March 701.
- In 1971, driving the raised-wing March 711, Peterson finished runner-up in the World Drivers' Championship behind Jackie Stewart, scoring five podiums (including 2nd places at Monaco, Silverstone, Monza, and Mosport) without winning a single race.
- Lost the historic 1971 Italian Grand Prix at Monza by a mere 0.01 seconds to Peter Gethin in the closest finish in Formula One history.

### The Lotus Years & Victory Spree (1973–1976)
- Joined Colin Chapman's Team Lotus for 1973 alongside reigning champion Emerson Fittipaldi. Peterson scored nine pole positions and took his **maiden Grand Prix victory at Circuit Paul Ricard** (French GP), followed by wins in Austria, Monza, and Watkins Glen.
- In 1974, despite the ageing Lotus 72, Peterson scored three brilliant victories at Monaco, Dijon-Prenois, and Monza.
- Returned to March in 1976, scoring a famous upset victory at Monza in the March 761.
- Spent 1977 with Tyrrell racing the six-wheeled Tyrrell P34, scoring a podium at Zolder (3rd).

### Return to Lotus & Tragic Monza Finale (1978)
- Rejoined Team Lotus for 1978 as teammate to Mario Andretti in the revolutionary ground-effect **Lotus 79**, signing a strict contract as team number-two driver.
- Loyally shadowed Andretti in four dominant 1-2 sweeps, taking magnificent victories in South Africa (Kyalami) and Austria (Österreichring).
- At the start of the Italian Grand Prix at Monza on September 10, 1978, Peterson was caught in a violent multi-car pileup triggered by premature starting lights. His Lotus 78 hit the Armco barriers head-on and burst into flames; he was pulled from the burning cockpit by James Hunt, Patrick Depailler, and Clay Regazzoni.
- Peterson suffered 27 fractures to his legs and feet. During surgery overnight at Niguarda Hospital in Milan, bone marrow fat entered his bloodstream, causing a fatal fat embolism.
- Peterson was crowned posthumous World Championship runner-up behind Mario Andretti.
