## Historical Significance

Sir Jackie Stewart is widely regarded as one of the greatest, most influential figures in motorsport history. A triple Formula One World Champion, Stewart held the all-time Grand Prix victory record (27 wins) from his retirement in 1973 until Alain Prost surpassed it in 1987. Beyond his sublime driving prowess, Stewart single-handedly revolutionized motorsport safety, transforming Grand Prix racing from a lethal bloodsport into a professionally regulated modern championship.

## Formula 1 Career Overview

### Early Years with BRM (1965–1967)
- Burst onto the scene in 1965 with Owen Racing Organisation (BRM) alongside Graham Hill, scoring a podium on his Monaco debut and winning the Italian Grand Prix at Monza in his rookie season.
- Survived a horrifying 150 mph crash in torrential rain at Spa-Francorchamps in 1966, trapped in his fuel-soaked cockpit for 25 minutes. This near-death ordeal sparked his lifelong campaign for circuit barriers, on-site medical teams, mandatory fireproof overalls, seatbelts, and full-face helmets.

### The Tyrrell Partnership & Matra World Championship (1968–1969)
- Formed a legendary bond with team owner Ken Tyrrell.
- In 1968, drove with a broken wrist in a plastic cast to conquer torrential rain at Zandvoort and scored his immortal 4-minute wet-weather victory at the Nürburgring Nordschleife in thick fog.
- In 1969, dominated in the Matra MS80 Ford Cosworth DFV, winning six Grands Prix (South Africa, Spain, Netherlands, France, Britain, Italy) to clinch his maiden World Drivers' Championship at Monza.

### The Tyrrell Racing Era & Triple World Champion (1970–1973)
- When Matra insisted on using their own V12 engine, Tyrrell and Stewart commissioned their own chassis, the Tyrrell 001 and 003.
- In 1971, Stewart dominated with six wins to claim his second World Drivers' Championship.
- In 1973, Stewart secured his third World Championship with five victories, including a masterclass at Monaco and the Nürburgring.
- Decided to retire after his scheduled 100th Grand Prix at Watkins Glen in October 1973. However, following the tragic death of his beloved teammate and protégé François Cevert in Saturday qualifying, Stewart and the Tyrrell team withdrew from the race, retiring on 99 starts with 27 wins.
