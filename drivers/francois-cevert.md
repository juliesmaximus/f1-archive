## Historical Significance

Albert François Cevert Goldenberg was one of the most charismatic, gifted, and beloved drivers of the early 1970s. With piercing blue eyes, aristocratic French charm, and prodigious driving talent, Cevert became an international superstar and the ideal teammate and protégé to triple World Champion Jackie Stewart at the Tyrrell Racing Organisation.

---

## Formula 1 Career Overview

### Arrival at Tyrrell (1970)
- Brought into Formula One by Ken Tyrrell mid-way through 1970 to replace the late Johnny Servoz-Gavin, making his debut at Zandvoort.
- Scored his first championship point in only his fifth Grand Prix at Monza, finishing 6th.

### Maiden Grand Prix Victory & Championship 3rd (1971)
- Drove alongside Jackie Stewart in the iconic blue Tyrrell 002/003 Ford Cosworth DFV, helping Tyrrell win the 1971 Constructors' Championship.
- Supported Stewart with four podium finishes (2nd in France and Germany).
- Scored his **maiden Formula One World Championship victory** at the 1971 United States Grand Prix at Watkins Glen, winning by 40 seconds over Jo Siffert and pocketing the record $50,000 winner's purse. He became only the second French driver after Maurice Trintignant to win a World Championship Grand Prix.
- Finished 3rd overall in the 1971 World Drivers' Championship behind Stewart and Ronnie Peterson.

### Peak Years & The Stewart Partnership (1972–1973)
- In 1972, scored two podiums (2nd at Nivelles and Watkins Glen) and finished 2nd overall in the 24 Hours of Le Mans in a Matra-Simca MS670.
- In 1973, Cevert was in peak form, taking six 2nd-place finishes, shadowing team leader Stewart in dominant 1-2 sweeps at Zolder, Monaco, and Zandvoort. Stewart privately resolved to retire after the season and pass the Tyrrell number-one status to Cevert.

### Tragic Accident at Watkins Glen (1973)
- On October 6, 1973, during Saturday morning qualifying for the United States Grand Prix at Watkins Glen, Cevert lost control of his Tyrrell 006 in the high-speed uphill Esses. The car struck the right-hand Armco barrier, ricocheted across the track, and hit the left barrier nose-first at 150 mph, breaking the barrier and causing catastrophic fatal injuries.
- Deeply grief-stricken, Jackie Stewart and the Tyrrell team withdrew from the race, Stewart retiring on the eve of his 100th Grand Prix start.
