# Mike Hawthorn — Career Statistics & Profile

John Michael "Mike" Hawthorn (10 April 1929 – 22 January 1959) was a British racing driver who became the **United Kingdom's first-ever Formula One World Drivers' Champion in 1958** driving for Scuderia Ferrari. Instantly recognizable by his trademark bow tie, flat cap, and blonde hair, Hawthorn burst onto the World Championship scene in 1952 driving a small Cooper-Bristol, later winning the 1955 24 Hours of Le Mans and defeating Stirling Moss for the 1958 World Championship by one point.

---

## Career Summary

- **World Drivers' Championships**: **1** (1958 — Britain's first World Champion)
- **First Grand Prix**: 1952 Belgian Grand Prix (Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps, Cooper-Bristol T20, finished 4th)
- **First Grand Prix Win**: 1953 French Grand Prix (Reims-Gueux, Ferrari 500 — "The Race of the Century", defeating Fangio by 1 foot)
- **Final Grand Prix Win**: 1958 French Grand Prix (Reims-Gueux, Ferrari Dino 246)
- **Final Grand Prix**: 1958 Moroccan Grand Prix (Ain-Diab, Casablanca, 1958-10-19, finished 2nd to clinch title)
- **Total Grand Prix Starts**: 45 (47 entries)
- **Total Grand Prix Wins**: **3** (1953 France, 1954 Spain, 1958 France)
- **Total Pole Positions**: **4**
- **Total Podiums**: **18**
- **Total Career Points**: 112.64 (gross) / 127.64 (including dropped scores)

---

## 1952 Debut Season
In his maiden World Championship campaign in 1952, Hawthorn astonished the Grand Prix establishment by qualifying and racing the modest 2.0-litre Cooper-Bristol against factory Ferraris and Gordinis:
- Finished 4th on debut at Spa.
- Finished 3rd on the podium at Silverstone.
- Finished 4th at Zandvoort.
- Finished 5th in the 1952 World Drivers' Championship on 10 points, earning a factory Ferrari contract from Enzo Ferrari for 1953.
