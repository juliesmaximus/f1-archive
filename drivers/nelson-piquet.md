# Driver Profile: Nelson Piquet

## Overview & Historic Legacy
Nelson Piquet Souto Maior (born August 17, 1952) is a Brazilian racing icon and **three-time FIA Formula One World Drivers' Champion** (1981, 1983, 1987). Widely celebrated as one of motorsport's shrewdest technical minds and fiercest competitors, Piquet was the defining driver of Brabham's Gordon Murray era and the first driver in Formula One history to win the World Championship in a turbocharged car (1983).

Over a 14-season career spanning 204 starts, Piquet claimed 23 victories, 60 podiums, and 24 pole positions. His tactical brilliance, technical mastery of vehicle setup, and groundbreaking testing work on tire warmers and active suspension fundamentally shaped modern Grand Prix racing.

## Formula One Career

### Rise with Brabham & Maiden Titles (1978–1985)
- Signed by Bernie Ecclestone in late 1978 to partner Niki Lauda at Parmalat Brabham.
- **Maiden Win (1980 US GP West, Long Beach):** In the Gordon Murray-designed Brabham BT49 Ford Cosworth DFV, Piquet scored a Grand Slam (pole, win, fastest lap, led every lap), finishing runner-up in the 1980 World Championship with 3 wins.
- **1981 World Championship:** In a nail-biting season finale at Caesars Palace in Las Vegas, Piquet overcame extreme heat exhaustion to finish 5th, clinching his **first World Drivers' Championship** by one point over Carlos Reutemann (50 to 49 points).
- **1983 World Championship (First Turbo Champion):** Piloting the dart-shaped Brabham BT52 powered by the Paul Rosche-designed BMW M12/13 1.5-litre turbocharged 4-cylinder engine, Piquet won in Brazil, Monza, and Brands Hatch, overhauling Alain Prost at the Kyalami finale to become the **first turbo World Champion in F1 history**.

### The Williams-Honda Dynasty & The Mansell Rivalry (1986–1987)
- Moved to Williams Grand Prix Engineering in 1986 to partner Nigel Mansell with potent Honda turbo V6 power.
- **1986 Battle:** Piquet won 4 races, entering Adelaide in a three-way showdown where Prost emerged champion after Mansell's tire explosion.
- **1987 World Championship:** Despite suffering a terrifying 180 mph practice crash at Tamburello (Imola) that caused permanent concussion and depth perception issues, Piquet used intellectual supremacy, mechanical sympathy, and early active suspension to score 11 podiums and 3 wins, defeating Mansell to capture his **third World Drivers' Championship**.

### Lotus & Benetton Finale (1988–1991)
- Drove for Lotus in 1988–1989 before joining Benetton under Flavio Briatore and John Barnard.
- Won back-to-back races at Suzuka and Adelaide in 1990, and took his 23rd and final victory at the 1991 Canadian Grand Prix in Montreal when Mansell stalled on the final lap.
