## Historical Significance

Andreas Nikolaus "Niki" Lauda is an immortal titan of Formula One. A triple World Drivers' Champion, Lauda remains the **only driver in history to win World Championships for both Scuderia Ferrari and McLaren**. Renowned for his analytical mind, clinical precision, and blunt honesty—earning him the nickname *"The Computer"*—Lauda's courage after surviving his near-fatal fiery inferno at the Nürburgring in 1976 and returning to race 42 days later stands as one of the greatest feats of human bravery in all of sporting history.

## Formula 1 Career Overview

### Early Years & Ferrari Dominance (1971–1977)
- Took bank loans to buy drives with March (1971–1972) and BRM (1973).
- Signed by Enzo Ferrari for 1974 on the recommendation of teammate Clay Regazzoni. Scored his maiden win at Jarama (Spain) and won again at Zandvoort.
- In 1975, dominated in Mauro Forghieri's revolutionary Ferrari 312T with transverse gearbox, winning five Grands Prix to capture his **first World Drivers' Championship** and Ferrari's first title in 11 years.
- In 1976, dominated early with five wins. On August 1 at the Nürburgring Nordschleife, suffered a horrific 140 mph crash at Bergwerk; trapped in a blazing cockpit, he suffered third-degree burns to his head and face and inhaled toxic gases, receiving the Catholic Last Rites in hospital.
- Incredibly, missing only two races, Lauda returned to race at Monza just 42 days later, his wounds bleeding through his bandages, finishing an astonishing 4th! Withdrew from the torrential Fuji finale on safety grounds, conceding the title to James Hunt by a single point.
- In 1977, dominated through calm tactical brilliance to win his **second World Championship** for Ferrari before walking out after relations with Enzo Ferrari soured.

### Brabham, Retirement & The McLaren Comeback (1978–1985)
- Drove for Bernie Ecclestone's Brabham in 1978–1979, winning the 1978 Swedish GP in the famous Brabham BT46B "Fan Car" before abruptly retiring in practice at Canada in 1979, stating he was "bored of driving in circles."
- Founded Lauda Air, but was lured back to Formula One by Ron Dennis and McLaren in 1982, winning his third race back at Long Beach.
- In 1984, driving the Porsche-built TAG Turbo McLaren MP4/2, Lauda defeated teammate Alain Prost in the closest championship finish in history, taking his **third World Championship** by just **half a point (0.5 pts)** (72 to 71.5 points).
- Retired permanently from driving at the end of 1985 with 25 wins.

### Leadership Legacy
- Served as senior advisor to Ferrari, Jaguar team principal, and from 2012 until his death as Non-Executive Chairman of Mercedes-AMG Petronas F1 Team, playing the pivotal role in signing Lewis Hamilton and orchestrating Mercedes' historic dominance.
